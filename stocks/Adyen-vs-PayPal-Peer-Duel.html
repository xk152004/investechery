The Call
Adyen is the stronger business for the next 5 to 10 years: its growth comes from enterprise cells it is still taking share in, and it turns each euro of net revenue into more operating profit than PayPal does.
The load-bearing fact: Adyen's net revenue after pass-through costs rose from EUR 1,001.5m in 2021 to EUR 2,364.2m in 2025 (+24% a year), while PayPal's transaction margin dollars rose from $14.0bn to $15.5bn (+2.5% a year), and Adyen still earned 46.9% EBIT on that base versus PayPal's 39.2% (ESEF FY2022 and FY2025; 10-K FY2023 and FY2025).

The Three Answers

1. Who has the stronger growth profile, by product x geography?
Adyen, clear

Three Adyen cells carry the next five years. (1) Unified Commerce, where large retailers and food chains move store and online payments onto one platform: 486 customers already process at least EUR 10m on both channels, and 838K terminals are live (+27%). (2) Platforms, where software companies embed payments, cards and loans for their small-business users: 37 platforms now process over EUR 1bn a year, up from 32. (3) North America, +30% CC in H1 2026, driven by the same share-of-wallet mechanism: Adyen's share of a merchant's volume typically climbs from under 20% in years 3 to 7 to over 40% after year 12, so today's cohort funds growth for years (Adyen H1 2026 letter). PayPal's forward engine is narrower. Management says financial services (Venmo, BNPL, credit) will be the largest driver of future transaction margin growth, and PSP volume grew 13%. But online branded checkout, over half of profit dollars, is guided to low-single-digit volume growth for 2026, and 2027 targets were withdrawn (PayPal Q4 2025 and Q2 2026 calls). The new growth is either thin-margin processing or balance-sheet credit.

2. Who converts that growth into superior margins?
Adyen, clear on level, narrow on direction

Adyen converts through architecture. One codebase and one ledger mean each new channel, region or product runs on infrastructure it already pays for, so operating costs fell from 58.1% to 53.1% of net revenue over FY2023 to FY2025 while net revenue rose 45% (ESEF FY2023 to FY2025). PayPal's conversion has been driven by cost cuts, not mix. Its EBIT on transaction margin moved from 36.7% to 39.2%, and 2023 included a $339m gain on selling Happy Returns (10-K FY2025). Its growth cells dilute margin: Braintree carries higher transaction expense rates, and branded checkout is buying presentment with merchant incentives that cut take rate by 7bps to 1.61% (Q2 2026 call). The direction is closer than the level. Adyen guides 2026 EBITDA margin about 1pt below 2025 because of the acquisitions, while PayPal targets at least $1.5bn of gross run-rate savings over two to three years. So PayPal's margin trend could improve while Adyen's pauses. Adyen's 2028 target of above 55% EBITDA is the test (H1 2026 letter).

Basis: Adyen net revenue; PayPal transaction margin dollars, both revenue after pass-through costs. Adyen operating costs = wages, social charges, D&A and other operating expenses (EUR 1,255.3m); PayPal = customer support, sales and marketing, technology and development, G&A, restructuring ($9,400m). Reported gross margins are left out because Adyen books interchange net and PayPal books it gross. Sources: ESEF FY2025; 10-K FY2025.
3. Where do the vulnerabilities sit if the tide turns?
PayPal breaks first

PayPal's exposure is credit plus consumer spending. It holds $5.5bn of consumer loans and $1.8bn of merchant loans. Merchant net charge-offs rose from 5.3% to 7.3% during 2025, and revenue share from its US credit partner drops to zero if returns fall below a minimum threshold. On top of that, about $1.1bn of 2026 transaction margin is interest on customer balances, which shrinks when rates fall (10-K FY2025; Q2 2026 guidance). A downturn would hit volume, losses and interest income together, and the core branded checkout franchise is already growing below the market. Adyen's exposure is different: pricing tiers. The largest merchants earn lower rates as they grow, and take rate fell from 17.1bps in H2 2025 to 16.2bps in H1 2026. In 2025 one large customer cut processed volume growth from 21% to 8%. With 300 merchants still driving about 60% of growth, a slowdown at a handful of enterprises would show up quickly (H2 2025 and H1 2026 letters). But Adyen carries little credit risk on its balance sheet (inferred: bad debts and provisions were EUR 16.1m in H1 2026, mostly one counterparty impairment and a legacy account clean-up), and its costs are mostly people it chooses to hire (550 to 650 net new in 2026). PayPal breaks first because three shocks can land together.

Segment growth engines

Unified Commerce (Adyen). Growth comes from moving existing online customers into their stores and signing multi-country retailers. Examples: Starbucks in 943 European stores, LVMH, Lufthansa. The cost is terminals and implementation teams. The driver lasts because merchants that consolidate channels share customer identity data across them, which a store-only or online-only rival cannot offer (H2 2025 letter). PayPal has no equivalent enterprise product. Its omnichannel push is consumer-side debit and tap-to-pay (Q2 2026 call).

Enterprise online (Adyen Digital vs Braintree). Both win large e-commerce and subscription merchants. Adyen's Digital growth ex the single large-volume customer was 11% by volume in H2 2025 and reaccelerated to +15% CC revenue in H1 2026. Braintree renegotiated or dropped unprofitable merchants in H1 2025, then grew volume mid-teens (10-K FY2025; Q2 2026 call). Braintree's revenue rose about $150m in 2025 on a base PayPal does not disclose.

Consumer money (PayPal). Venmo monetizes through its debit card and Pay with Venmo: users of both generate more than 9x the revenue per account of P2P-only users. BNPL grows through upstream presentment at merchants like Temu and Home Depot Canada. This is organic, but funded partly by the balance sheet: $1.7bn of BNPL receivables were held for sale at year-end 2025 under forward-flow agreements (Q2 2026 call; 10-K FY2025).

Acquired growth. Adyen's Talon.One (EUR 750m, about EUR 60m ARR, growing 30 to 40%) and Orb closed on 1 July 2026. They add about 1pt to 2026 growth and 1 to 2pts to 2027, so read 2026 organic growth as 20 to 22% CC (Talon.One call; H1 2026 letter). PayPal's January 2026 purchase of Cymbio (agentic commerce) is immaterial to growth (Wikipedia, PayPal article).

Insight: Adyen's organic engine is recurring share-of-wallet expansion: two thirds of H1 2026 growth came from customers onboarded in 2024 or earlier. Implication: Growth is visible 6 to 12 months out and does not need new logos. KPI: share of growth from the top 300 merchants, now about 60%, falling further by H2 2027. Source: Adyen H1 2026 letter.

Price control and route-to-market

Adyen sells directly to enterprises and platforms with volume-tiered pricing. It lowers its own price as merchants scale: take rate fell to 16.2bps in H1 2026 from 16.8bps a year earlier. It gives price to buy volume it can retain. PayPal's net price is set at two doors. Merchants pay a percentage fee, and PayPal is now funding "calculated deals" with strategic merchants (nearly 25% of branded checkout volume) and co-marketing for better button placement. Transaction take rate fell 7bps to 1.61% in Q2 2026, and the investments cost about 3 points of 2026 TM$ growth (Q4 2025 and Q2 2026 calls).

Insight: Both are cutting price. Adyen does it for merchants whose volume is rising; PayPal does it to defend a slowing button. Implication: Adyen's discounts buy durable share of wallet, while PayPal's buy presentment that must be repaid each contract cycle. KPI: Adyen net revenue CC growth minus processed volume growth (H1 2026: 21% vs 24%, -3pts); PayPal transaction take rate ex hedges (Q2 2026: -7bps YoY). Sources: Adyen H1 2026 letter; PayPal Q2 2026 call.

Competitive context

In enterprise acceptance, both compete with other global processors and bank-owned acquirers. PayPal's 10-K names the category broadly (card processors, digital wallets, BNPL, real-time payment systems). Share data by cell is ND in the sources. Adyen describes itself as outgrowing the market because it solves more of each customer's problems (H1 2026 letter). PayPal's aim for branded checkout is "narrowing the gap with the market over time", which admits share loss today (Q4 2025 call). On this evidence Adyen is a share-taker in its three cells, PayPal a share-donor in branded checkout and a share-taker in Venmo and BNPL (inferred from growth vs stated market context).

Risks by segment

Unified Commerce: implementation capacity. Multi-country store rollouts need people and terminals, and Adyen plans 550 to 650 hires in 2026. Enterprise online: tiering and single-customer swings, as the 2025 volume drop from one customer showed. More exposed: Adyen. Branded checkout: agentic and in-app checkouts that bypass the web button. Management says agentic will not matter for 2026 growth, but it must become "the default payment option" as AI shopping scales. More exposed: PayPal. Consumer money: credit cycle and rates, as covered in Answer 3. More exposed: PayPal (Adyen H1 2026 letter; PayPal Q4 2025 call; 10-K FY2025).

On cells alone, Adyen has the higher-probability growth path. Its three enterprise cells grow 15 to 40% on compounding wallet share, while PayPal's highest-margin cell grows 2% and its growth cells carry thinner margins or credit risk.
PayPal owns the consumer side of the network; Adyen owns the merchant's plumbing. In 2026 the merchant is setting the terms.

Moats: what rivals cannot copy

Adyen

Single global stack plus own banking licence. Proof: 99.9999% uptime through 837m Black Friday / Cyber Monday transactions, recognizing about 95% of 400m shoppers across online and in-store (H2 2025 letter). Durability: High. It took a decade to build and cannot be bought quickly. Cross-channel identity data (Dynamic Identification). Proof: pilots lifted conversion up to 6% and cut payment costs up to 3%. Durability: Med-High (inferred), because it depends on staying the single provider across channels.

PayPal

Two-sided consumer network: 439m active accounts, 228m monthly actives. Proof: buyer and seller protection and a trusted brand; BNPL shown upstream lifts branded volume by more than 10% (10-K FY2025; Q4 2025 call). Durability: Med. The habit is weakening at the web checkout, with payment transactions per active account at 57.7 vs 60.6 in 2024, though ex-PSP engagement grew 7% in Q2 2026. Venmo P2P network. Proof: TPV up double digits for seven straight quarters. Durability: High in the US.

Insight: Both have real moats, but PayPal's is under attack in its most profitable use (web checkout), while Adyen's deepens as merchants add channels. Implication: Adyen's moat supports pricing discipline; PayPal is paying to defend its own. KPI: Adyen customers processing across channels at scale, 486 in H1 2026, rising; PayPal monthly active accounts growth above 1%. Sources: Adyen H1 2026 letter; PayPal Q2 2026 call.

Customers: who controls net price and access

Adyen: enterprise and platform merchants on tiered contracts. Big customers hold real bargaining power: 300 merchants drive about 60% of growth, and one large customer's volume shift in 2025 took total processed volume growth from 21% to 8%. The offset is switching cost, which rises with each added channel and product, and long-standing customers still expanding after a decade (H2 2025 and H1 2026 letters). PayPal: merchants can always offer other wallets. PayPal is paying for presentment through strategic-merchant deals and co-marketing, and its Braintree merchants can move volume, as the 2025 repricing showed (Q4 2025 call; 10-K FY2025). Consumers set PayPal's volume through button choice, which management calls "selection".

Insight: Adyen concedes price per unit to large customers but keeps them and grows with them. PayPal concedes price to win placement it once had by default. Implication: When demand softens, margin pressure lands on PayPal's branded checkout first. KPI: PayPal branded checkout TPV CC growth vs e-commerce growth; Adyen take rate holding at or above 15.5bps in H2 2026. Sources as above.

Suppliers: who absorbs shocks

Adyen passes scheme and interchange costs through to merchants: costs from financial institutions plus terminal costs were 11.5% of gross revenue in FY2025, and the ratio barely moved as volume grew. Its key supply risk is compute, handled by pre-buying 2027 capex in 2026 (ESEF FY2025; H1 2026 letter). PayPal absorbs funding costs: its transaction expense rate is 0.89% of TPV, and cheaper funding (balances, bank, own credit) is a margin lever it controls only partly. US credit depends on a partner institution whose revenue share can drop to zero, and BNPL growth depends on forward-flow buyers (10-K FY2025). Pass-through: Adyen Strong; PayPal Partial.

Insight: A card-cost or funding shock flows straight into PayPal's P&L and past Adyen's. Implication: PayPal's gross economics are cyclical in a way Adyen's are not. KPI: PayPal transaction and credit loss rate above 0.11% of TPV (the 2023 level) in 2027. Source: 10-K FY2025.

The causal gap

(1) Architecture. Adyen runs one platform; PayPal is unifying Braintree, PayPal Complete Payments and Hyperwallet and moving to the cloud. Impact: Major. Laggard fix: already underway under the $1.5bn savings plan, realistic horizon 2 to 3 years or more (Q2 2026 call). (2) Enterprise cross-channel presence. Adyen has 486 merchants at scale on both channels; PayPal has no enterprise POS. Impact: Major. Not closable with money inside 18 months, because it requires terminal estates and in-store implementations. (3) Consumer network, which cuts the other way. PayPal's 439m accounts and Venmo are an asset Adyen cannot replicate. Impact: Moderate for PayPal, since it has not stopped branded checkout decelerating. Gaps (1) and (2) favor Adyen and neither is money-buyable quickly, so the moat story holds.

Adyen holds the stronger power position. Its moat compounds with each channel a merchant adds, it passes supplier costs through, and its discounts buy retention; PayPal's network is real but now costs money to defend. Early warning: for Adyen, take rate below 15bps with no volume acceleration; for PayPal, monthly active accounts turning negative.
Adyen spends about 7 points less of each net-revenue dollar to run the business, every year of the three. The gap comes from one stack and no consumer marketing.

Mapping. Adyen reports costs by nature under IFRS (wages, social charges, D&A, other operating expenses). PayPal reports by function under US GAAP. No functional split exists for Adyen, so its R&D share is ND. "Pass-through costs" = Adyen's costs incurred from financial institutions, terminal cost of goods sold and interest expense, against IFRS revenue plus interest income; for PayPal, transaction expense plus transaction and credit losses, against net revenues. Adyen's revenue is net of interchange, while PayPal books payments gross as principal, so the pass-through and gross-margin rows are structurally not comparable. The duel runs on the two clean rows: operating costs and EBIT, both as % of net revenue (Adyen) or TM$ (PayPal). PayPal's 2023 includes a $339m Happy Returns gain in restructuring and other; excluding it, 2023 EBIT would be about 34.2% of TM$ (inferred: (5,028 - 339) / 13,704). Adyen's IFRS revenue presentation changed in FY2023 (the FY2023 file shows 2022 revenue of EUR 8,935.6m against EUR 1,863.4m for 2023), which is why gross ratios before 2023 are not used.

The structural gap

The persistent gap is operating cost per unit of net revenue. Adyen was lower in all three years (5.2 to 7.8pts), and the gap widened in FY2024 and FY2025 as Adyen's ratio fell 5pts while PayPal's fell 2.5pts. The mechanism comes from Steps 1 and 2. Adyen runs one platform and sells to enterprises through a direct sales force with little consumer marketing. PayPal funds consumer brand campaigns (sales and marketing up $282m in 2025, mostly advertising), global customer support ($1.7bn), and multiple technology stacks. This confirms the power map: the company that passes supplier costs through and grows inside existing customers carries a lighter cost base. Two caveats cut both ways. PayPal's TM$ includes about $1.1bn of interest on customer balances (2026 guide), which flatters its ratio; Adyen's EUR 267.6m of finance income on float sits below EBIT. And PayPal's $1.5bn savings plan targets exactly this gap, but management intends to reinvest a significant portion, so the savings may not reach the EBIT line (Q2 2026 call).

Adyen runs the leaner engine: about 7pts less operating cost per unit of net revenue in each of three years, structural to a single-platform, merchant-only model.

What would flip the call

  • Adyen's tiering outruns its share-of-wallet gains. If large merchants' price tiers keep cutting take rate (17.1bps H2 2025 to 16.2bps H1 2026) while volume growth slows, net revenue growth converges toward PayPal-like levels. Early warning: net revenue CC growth below processed volume growth by more than 5pts for two consecutive halves, or FY2027 net revenue CC growth below 17% (shareholder letters).
  • Adyen's first acquisitions and leadership change break its cost discipline. Talon.One and Orb bring separate stacks into a one-platform company, and the CFO left on 31 Aug 2026.
