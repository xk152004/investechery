The Call
Broadcom is the materially stronger business for the next five to ten years, and unusually, all three lenses agree rather than splitting.
The load-bearing evidence is the thing that cannot be bought in five years: Broadcom carries a 31.4bn dollar software annuity at 94% gross margin behind an AI franchise almost six times Marvell's entire data-centre business, while Marvell carries 2.3bn dollars of declining communications revenue behind an 81%-data-center business it is paying its largest customer 6.4% of its equity to expand.

The Three Answers

1. Who has the stronger growth profile, by product and geography?
Broadcom

The forward mechanism is a contracted, capacity-anchored program book, not a market-size story. Three cells carry the next five years. Custom XPU, roughly 42.3bn dollars in FY2026E, rests on six customers with gigawatt-denominated schedules disclosed to 2028: Google under a long-term agreement at multi-tens of billions annually, Anthropic at 1 GW in 2026 rising to 5 GW in 2027 and an incremental 10 GW in 2028, OpenAI at 1.3 GW in 2027 and over 5 GW in 2028, and Meta at three MTIA generations and 3 GW through 2028, with management stating supply is already secured for the 115bn dollar FY2027 and 230bn dollar FY2028 outlooks (AVGO Q3 FY2026 call, 2 Sep 2026). AI networking, roughly 15.7bn dollars, grows independently of that: Tomahawk 6 is deployed at hyperscalers that buy their accelerators elsewhere, and Tomahawk 7 at 200 Tbps is taped out. Infrastructure software, 31.4bn dollars at 94% segment gross margin and 15% ARR growth, compounds through any AI air pocket and funds the research budget inside one.

Marvell's forward mechanism is real and narrower. Scale-up optics and scale-up switching are genuinely new addressable market, its optical DSP franchise is the estimated share leader, and Celestial AI's photonic fabric already carries a design win at a top hyperscaler. But its raised FY2028 target of 18bn dollars is roughly 8% of Broadcom's FY2028 AI-only line of sight, and the gap is widening rather than closing.

2. Who converts that growth into superior margins, and through what?
Broadcom

There is no compounder-versus-fortress tension to report here, because the growth winner and the margin winner are the same company. The conversion mechanism is operating expense intensity, not pricing. Broadcom carries research and selling costs at roughly 7% of revenue consolidated and roughly 6% inside its semiconductor segment; Marvell carries them at 22%. That produced a 31.3-point operating margin gap in the latest comparable quarters, 67.9% against 36.6%, on a gross margin gap of only 16.1 points. Strip the software annuity out entirely and the point sharpens: Broadcom's semiconductor segment earned a 66.9% gross margin against Marvell's 58.9%, a gap of 8 points, and converted it into a 61.3% operating margin against Marvell's 36.6%, a gap of 24.7.

The tension worth naming cuts the other way. Broadcom's gross margin is deteriorating precisely because of the mix that drives its growth: 78% a year ago, 75% in Q3 FY2026, guided to approximately 73% in Q4, as rising XPU memory content passes through at little markup. Marvell's sits flat at 58 to 59% with custom mix as its own stated headwind. The two are converging on gross margin and diverging on operating margin. What that says is that custom silicon is a structurally lower-margin business for whoever sells it, so Broadcom's advantage does not rest on a pricing moat in accelerators at all. It rests on a relative cost base one-third the size and an annuity Marvell has no version of, which is the harder gap to close, because Marvell cannot fix it by winning a design contest.

3. Where does each break if the tide turns?
Marvell breaks first

Broadcom's exposure is concentration compounded by financial entanglement. Its top five end customers are approximately 40% of net revenue, its AI franchise sits on six accelerator customers, and Anthropic alone becomes the largest of them in 2027. Through the AI XPV Platform established with Apollo and Blackstone in June 2026, with a first 35bn dollar tranche closed for Anthropic's 1 GW deployment, Broadcom is now a participant in its customers' financing and carries residual value guarantees it describes as low-risk contingent liabilities. If frontier-lab funding tightens, its order book and its contingent liabilities move in the same direction at the same time. Against that it holds a 31.4bn dollar software annuity uncorrelated with AI capital spending and 27.5bn dollars of annual operating cash flow. It has a floor.

Marvell has the same concentration without the floor. Distributor A was 44% of revenue in Q2 FY2027, up from 34% a year earlier; four customers were 72% of gross accounts receivable at 1 August 2026; data center is 79% of revenue and nothing sits behind it, because communications and other is 2.3bn dollars, guided down low-to-mid teens percent in Q3 FY2027, and the automotive Ethernet line was sold. Marvell has also been buying its seat, with a 58.97m-share warrant to Google and 3.5bn dollars for Celestial AI inside seven months; the share components of the two together are roughly 12% of the diluted count. A single program loss at Distributor A or the largest custom socket removes roughly a quarter of company revenue against a fixed research base of 25% of sales that is already calibrated to a custom business management expects to more than double in FY2028.

Two companies riding the same AI wave, but one is selling four things and the other is selling one.

Before any cell table, one normalization that matters more than the usual label mapping. Broadcom reports two segments and discloses AI semiconductor revenue as a management metric; Marvell reports a single operating segment and, since December 2025, only two end markets. Neither discloses revenue by product and geography together, and both state in their own filings that their geographic tables do not indicate end demand. Broadcom: for the majority of its products, title and control transfer to customers in Penang, Malaysia. Marvell: a substantial majority of shipments to China are for non-China customers with contract manufacturing there. Building a product-by-country grid from those tables would fabricate precision, so the cells below run on product with a stated demand geography, and the geographic tables are carried separately with the honest reading attached.

The second thing to fix before comparing is scale. Broadcom's guided FY2026 revenue of roughly 105.9bn dollars is about 8.8 times Marvell's guided FY2027 revenue of roughly 12.0bn dollars. Absolutes appear below for context, but every judgment rests on ratios, growth rates and mechanisms.

Segment growth engines

In custom accelerators, Broadcom's driver is a contracted program book disclosed in unusual specificity, and it is organic: Broadcom has made no semiconductor acquisition since 2019. Marvell's driver is real but undisclosed in dollars, and its cost is now explicit. On 18 August 2026 Marvell issued Google a warrant over 58,970,907 shares at 206 dollars, vesting in 240 tranches, one for each 500m dollars of Custom Products revenue through FY2033. In AI networking and optics, Marvell is at or near parity: the Inphi-derived optical DSP franchise is the estimated share leader, 800G is in volume and 1.6T is ramping, and COLORZ 1600 coherent pluggables address a scale-across opportunity management sizes at more than ten times current front-end DCI bandwidth. Broadcom answers with switching, and with laser fabs it owns. In infrastructure software Marvell has no analogue at all, and in non-AI semiconductors Marvell has been shrinking its own optionality, having divested automotive Ethernet for 2.5bn dollars in net proceeds.

Insight: Broadcom's accelerator growth is bought with engineering, described by management as the fastest time to market from product definition to production without the need for respins; Marvell's is now partly bought with equity. Implication: Broadcom's incremental accelerator dollar converts fully to revenue while Marvell's converts at a discount that scales with success, because warrant vesting is recognized as a reduction of revenue. KPI: number of Google warrant tranches vested, first disclosable in the MRVL FY2027 Q3 10-Q (~Dec 2026); each tranche marks 500m dollars of custom revenue recognized. [Sources: AVGO Q3 FY2026 call, 2 Sep 2026; MRVL 8-K, 19 Aug 2026; MRVL FY2026 10-K]

Competitive context

Published share estimates for merchant custom AI silicon disagree by definition but agree on direction: Counterpoint Research (26 January 2026) puts Broadcom at approximately 60% and Marvell at approximately 8% of the compute-ASIC design-partner market by 2027, while a J.P. Morgan estimate reported second-hand (22 June 2026) puts Broadcom at 80 to 85% and Marvell at 10 to 12% of the high-end ASIC market. Alchip is entering the AWS Trainium supply chain and MediaTek is attacking the Google socket with the v8x inference part; Broadcom stated on 2 September 2026 that it is shipping TPU v8i ahead of MediaTek's v8t, which was initiated earlier. Vendor shares for merchant switch silicon, PAM4 DSP and PCIe retimers are ND: 650 Group, Dell'Oro, LightCounting and Cignal AI publish these to subscribers only, and the available system-level proxy understates Broadcom because Arista and Cisco whitebox platforms consume merchant silicon. Read as share direction, Broadcom is a share-taker in custom accelerators, having added OpenAI and Anthropic programs in eighteen months, and a defender in switching against a vertically integrated Nvidia; Marvell is a share-donor in custom accelerators on the Counterpoint estimate and a share-holder in optical DSP.

Moats: what rivals cannot copy

Broadcom

Respin-free leading-edge execution. Durability: High. Proof rather than adjective: Broadcom shipped Google's TPU v8i into production ahead of MediaTek's competing v8t, which was initiated earlier, and attributes this to the fastest time to market from product definition to production without the need for respins. A respin on a leading-edge accelerator costs a customer two quarters it cannot buy back.

Owned IP and process assets across the interconnect chain. Durability: High for process, Medium for IP. SerDes, chip-to-chip interconnect, leading-edge HBM and SRAM integration and advanced packaging, backed by owned fabs for FBAR filters and GaAs and InP lasers, plus a Singapore substrate fab deploying from fiscal 2027.

A switching installed base independent of the accelerator socket. Durability: Medium. Tomahawk 6 is deployed at effectively all AI hyperscalers building accelerators with Broadcom and at those that are not. Nvidia Spectrum-X is a well-funded vertically integrated attack.

A software annuity with real switching costs. Durability: High. VMware Cloud Foundation under enterprise-wide licences, 94% segment gross margin, ARR growing 15%, uncorrelated with the semiconductor cycle.

Marvell

Optical DSP leadership. Durability: Medium-High. The Inphi franchise is the estimated share leader at approximately 70% on the one independent estimate located (BEP Research, 7 March 2026, a single analyst source and indicative only), with 800G in volume and 1.6T ramping. Broadcom, Credo and captive hyperscaler programs all attack it, and Broadcom now claims leadership in leading-edge optical DSPs on its own call.

Breadth across every scale-up option. Durability: Medium. Marvell is positioned across all three purpose-built scale-up protocols, UALink, ESUN and NVLink Fusion through an expanded Nvidia partnership, and across all three modulator technologies with both NPO and CPO packaging. Optionality is not yet a moat; it becomes one only if a branch wins.

Celestial AI photonic fabric. Durability: Medium, unproven. Acquired 2 February 2026 for 3.5bn dollars of total consideration. Proof it is more than a technology bet: Celestial had already secured a design win with one of the world's largest hyperscalers for PF chiplets co-packaged into both custom accelerators and scale-up switches. Guided revenue is a 500m dollar annualized run rate only by Q4 FY2028.

The XPU-attach category. Durability: Low-Medium. CXL, custom NICs, storage and memory interface controllers and near-memory compute. This is adjacency built on relationship access, and the August 2026 warrant shows what that access costs.

Insight: Broadcom's two strongest moats are years-to-replicate; Marvell's strongest moat is genuinely defensible but its second and third are option value with one disclosed design win between them. Implication: in a downturn Broadcom defends on installed base and contract while Marvell defends on being technically necessary in a transition that has not yet happened. KPI: a second named hyperscaler design win for Celestial AI photonic fabric by the end of FY2028 - without it, the acquisition is a single-customer bet that cost roughly 6% dilution. [Sources: AVGO Q3 FY2026 call and FY2025 10-K; MRVL Q3 FY2026 call, Q2 FY2027 call and FY2027 Q2 10-Q]

Customers: who controls net price and access

On concentration the two look superficially alike and are not. Broadcom's top five end customers are approximately 40% of net revenue and distributors 48%; Marvell's Distributor A alone was 44% of net revenue in Q2 FY2027, up from 34% a year earlier, Direct Customer A was 14% in FY2026, and four customers were 72% of gross accounts receivable at 1 August 2026. On access quality the gap is wider than the percentages: Broadcom holds a long-term agreement with Google covering future generations of TPUs and AI networking at multi-tens of billions of dollars annually, plus gigawatt-denominated commitments from Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta running to 2028, while Marvell's access is design-win by design-win and its most significant scope expansion was formalized in a warrant agreement.

That warrant, in full, is the exhibit. On 18 August 2026 Marvell issued Google a warrant over 58,970,907 shares at 206 dollars, exercisable to 18 August 2033. A time-based portion vests quarterly over the first year; the remainder vests in 240 equal tranches, one for each 500m dollars of Custom Products revenue purchased by or on behalf of Google through FY2033. Against roughly 921m diluted shares, full vesting is about 6.4% dilution, and full vesting implies roughly 120bn dollars of cumulative custom revenue. It is not the first: Marvell issued a 4.2m-share customer warrant in FY2025 and a 1.0m-share customer warrant in FY2026, both recognized as reductions to revenue as qualifying revenue is recognized. Broadcom's FY2025 10-K and FY2026 Q3 10-Q contain no customer warrant.

Insight: in the same industry and the same quarter, Broadcom is raising realized price on a 94%-margin software base while Marvell is handing its largest customer equity that reduces reported revenue as that customer buys more. Implication: Marvell's marginal custom dollar is worth less than a headline dollar and the discount grows with success, which is part of why revenue up 42% in FY2026 produced operating cash flow up 4%. KPI: Marvell warrant tranches vested, first disclosable in the FY2027 Q3 10-Q (~Dec 2026); two or more by Q4 FY2027 validates the custom ramp and confirms the revenue offset is live. [Sources: MRVL 8-K 19 Aug 2026, FY2026 10-K, FY2027 Q2 10-Q; AVGO FY2025 10-K and Q3 FY2026 call]

Suppliers: who absorbs shocks

Both are bottlenecked in the same place. Broadcom names leading-edge wafers, substrates and HBM memory and states supply is secured for its FY2027 and FY2028 outlooks; Marvell names pervasive industry-wide constraints. The difference is what each is doing about it. Broadcom holds Medium vertical integration, outsourcing a majority of manufacturing but owning FBAR and laser fabs and deploying a Singapore substrate fab from FY2027. Marvell holds Low vertical integration, is purely fabless with foundries located in Taiwan, and is making approximately 1bn dollars of capacity prepayments to suppliers in FY2027. Pass-through strength is Partial for both, evidenced in gross margin: Broadcom's fell from 78% to a guided 73% on rising accelerator memory content while its operating margin held at 66%, and Marvell's sits at roughly 59% with custom mix its stated headwind.

Insight: Broadcom is answering the substrate bottleneck with a fab it will own from FY2027 while Marvell is answering it by prepaying third parties approximately 1bn dollars. Implication: when allocation tightens again Broadcom's position improves structurally and Marvell's improves only as long as it keeps writing cheques. Where the growth map and the power map disagree, and it matters: Marvell's fastest-growing and most-promoted cell, custom silicon, is precisely the cell where it has the least pricing power. It is the cell that carries a 44%-of-revenue distributor, the cell whose mix management names as the gross-margin headwind, and the cell it had to buy scope in with a 59m-share warrant. Its strongest power position, optical DSP, is the cell it discusses least in dollar terms and the one where Broadcom owns the underlying laser manufacture. The company is growing hardest where it is weakest and is quietest where it is strongest.

The causal gap

1. Scale of the design-and-supply base. Impact: Major. Broadcom runs approximately 33,000 employees, 57% of them in research and development, against a revenue base guided to 105.9bn dollars. Marvell spends 25.3% of sales on research to cover optical, switching, custom, storage and communications simultaneously. Closing this needs either a revenue base several times larger, which takes a decade, or a narrower product scope, which forfeits the breadth that is its stated differentiator. Not closable in five years.

2. Who pays whom for access. Impact: Major. Broadcom holds a long-term supply agreement with Google and takes no dilution; Marvell issued Google a warrant over 6.4% of its diluted shares to expand the same relationship. Closing this requires Marvell to become technically non-substitutable in a socket, which is what Celestial AI is for, and not before FY2029.

3. Presence or absence of a counter-cyclical annuity. Impact: Moderate to Major. Broadcom has 31.4bn dollars of 94%-gross-margin software growing 15% ARR; Marvell has 2.3bn dollars of declining communications revenue after divesting automotive Ethernet. Marvell could buy an annuity, but three years of capital allocation have run the other way. Not closable without reversing stated strategy.

The honesty test: could a well-funded rival close each gap in eighteen months with money alone? For gaps one and three, no. For gap two, no, because it is a consequence of the other two.

Broadcom holds the stronger power position on all three levers, but the supplier lever is close and the optical socket is genuinely contested, which is where Marvell's case has to be made.
The gap that decides this duel is not gross margin, where the two are converging. It is operating expense intensity, where they are not.

Both companies present costs by function, so the usual by-nature translation problem does not arise. The real reconciliation issue is larger: both income statements are dominated by acquisition-related intangible amortization, sitting in different proportions in different lines. Broadcom's FY2025 cost of revenue carries 6,031m dollars of it against a total of 20,593m, with a further 2,031m in operating expenses. Marvell's FY2026 total is 942.0m, of which approximately 641.4m sits in cost of goods sold, derived as cost of goods sold of 4,013.9m less material, labour and other product cost of 3,322.8m, less stock compensation in cost of goods sold of 49.2m, less restructuring in cost of goods sold of 0.5m. Comparing the two on GAAP alone therefore flatters neither consistently, so three views follow. All three tell the same story with different noise, which is the honesty check.

One further flag before the tables. Marvell's FY2025 cost of goods sold includes 357.9m dollars of restructuring-related impairment of acquired intangibles, inventory and equipment, which the company itemizes; that year's gross margin is depressed by roughly 6.2 points for a one-off reason. It is left in the GAAP view and removed in the quarterly view. Fiscal years are offset by roughly one quarter throughout: Broadcom FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 ended 29 October 2023, 3 November 2024 and 2 November 2025; Marvell FY2024, FY2025 and FY2026 ended 3 February 2024, 1 February 2025 and 31 January 2026.

The structural gap

The most persistent gap is operating expense intensity, present in every one of the three years and in the latest quarter. Broadcom carries research, development and selling costs at roughly 7% of revenue consolidated and roughly 6% inside its semiconductor segment; Marvell carries them at 22%. The mechanism comes straight from the growth map: Broadcom amortizes an 11bn dollar research budget over a 106bn dollar revenue base and a small number of very large sockets, while Marvell spends a quarter of every sales dollar covering optical, switching, custom, storage and communications simultaneously on a 12bn dollar base.

Does the cost engine confirm the power map? Partly, and the disagreement is the more useful half. It confirms it on operating margin and on cash conversion, both of which track Broadcom's contractual position and Marvell's cheque-writing. It complicates it on gross margin, where the two are converging rather than diverging: Broadcom's slide from 78% to a guided 73% on accelerator memory content says that even the strong party in this industry passes through memory cost at little markup, and that custom silicon is a structurally lower-margin business for whoever sells it. The corollary is that Broadcom's advantage in this duel does not rest on a pricing moat in custom silicon at all. It rests on a relative cost base and a software annuity Marvell has no version of, which is a harder gap to close than a price gap, because Marvell cannot fix it by winning a design contest.

Broadcom runs the leaner engine on every measure and has done through three years and a restructuring cycle, but the gap that matters is expense intensity and cash conversion, not gross margin, where the two are converging.

What would flip the call

  • Scale-up goes optical faster than copper roadmaps assume, and Celestial AI's photonic fabric wins more than one hyperscaler. Broadcom's scale-up answer, Tomahawk Ultra and SUE-T, is Ethernet-and-copper centric; Marvell's is an acquired 16 Tb/s photonic-fabric chiplet already designed into one top hyperscaler's accelerators and switches, freeing die-edge beachfront for HBM in a way edge-connected CPO cannot. Early warning: a second named PF-chiplet hyperscaler design win, and Celestial tracking to its guided 500m dollar annualized run rate by Q4 FY2028. First checkable at the Marvell Investor Day, 6 October 2026.
  • One of Broadcom's six accelerator customers insources, re-awards, or slips its gigawatt schedule. MediaTek is already contesting the Google socket with v8x and Alchip is entering AWS Trainium, so the competitive set for custom silicon is widening exactly as Broadcom's disclosed outlook extends further out. Early warning: any revision at all to the 115bn dollar FY2027 or 230bn dollar FY2028 AI outlook. Management said explicitly it does not intend to update these quarterly, so any update is signal. First opportunity: Q4 FY2026 results, around December 2026.
  • The frontier-lab financing chain impairs and the AI XPV Platform stops being off-balance-sheet in substance. Broadcom closed a first 35bn dollar tranche with Apollo and Blackstone in June 2026 for Anthropic's 1 GW deployment and may provide residual value guarantees it treats as low-risk contingent liabilities; that makes its order book and its liabilities correlated to the same event. Early warning: the size and terms of tranches after the first 35bn dollars, and whether residual value guarantees appear as recognized liabilities rather than contingent disclosure in Broadcom's FY2026 10-K, due around December 2026.

Where to spend your time
Broadcom first, but not on the part everyone is reading: the accelerator pipeline is already disclosed in more detail than most managements give, and the thin disclosure, the only place where the downside is structural rather than cyclical, is the AI XPV Platform and its residual value guarantees in the FY2026 10-K due around December 2026. The second call on your time is Marvell's Investor Day on 6 October 2026, which should put the first dollar figure on the custom business and on scale-up optics: Marvell is the more interesting research problem and the weaker business, and that is the day you would find out whether the photonic-fabric transition is real and early.

Method. This is a three-lens peer duel: a growth and profitability map that breaks both companies out of consolidated totals into normalized product cells ranked by contribution, a power map comparing moats, customer relationships and supplier links, and a cost engine comparing three years of five expense ratios with the acquisition-amortization and fiscal-offset reconciliations stated rather than buried. The 80/20 filter keeps only the cells that carry substantially all of the combined economics. All figures are in USD as reported by each company with no FX conversion; fiscal years are offset by roughly one quarter and every table says so. ND means the data does not exist publicly; (inferred) means a figure derived by stated arithmetic from disclosed numbers, never an invented one. This is a business-strength comparison, not a valuation. It contains no view on either company's share price, no multiple, no fair value and no recommendation, and says nothing about what either stock is worth.
