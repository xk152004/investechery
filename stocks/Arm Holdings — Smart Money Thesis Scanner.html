Arm is framed as an AI exposure that is "not overly dependent on the current cycle of AI capital formation" (4Q25).

"ARM's royalty-based business model limits its revenue variability" — 4Q25 commentary (cited as both the defensive attraction and the reason it lagged).

"ARM benefited from rising interest in CPUs which are set to take on a larger role with agentic AI, and from expectations about the adoption of ARM's own AGI CPU chip which would allow the company to capture improved economics." — 2Q26. 1Q26 cites a "burgeoning royalty revenue stream in the data center."
Fundamental drivers
Custom-silicon proliferation (royalty base) · data-centre CPU royalties · own-silicon (AGI CPU) economics — all per quotes above.

not addressed by this investor

not addressed by this investor

Low revenue leverage to the AI build-out and timing of own silicon: "its own-silicon initiatives are still a year or two away." — 4Q25.

4. Consensus & Divergence

Where investors agree

Seven of nine substantive cards (Artisan, Baillie Gifford, SGA, Sands, Tran, Liontrust, SoftBank) own Arm for the same reason: agentic AI is pushing up data-centre CPU demand, and Arm's low-power architecture wins that share. Five (Artisan, Baillie Gifford, Tran, NZS, SoftBank) add the 2026 own-silicon (AGI CPU) launch as a step-change in value capture. Almost every fund that writes about Arm trimmed or capped the position in Q2 2026 after the ~+133% quarter: Baillie Gifford halved it, SGA cut to below-average weight, Liontrust cut ~60%, NZS went 'back to optionality', and Artisan held at ~3%.

Where investors split

Royalty compounder (2025-vintage thesis)
SGA U.S. Large Cap Growth / Global Growth, Liontrust Global Technology / Global Innovation, Tran Capital Midcap / Multi-Cap Growth
Value per device rises through v9/CSS royalty rates, recurring revenue and pricing power. SGA calls Arm 'a high-quality, long-term compounder'; Liontrust's 2025 case is 'more complexity per chip, more chips shipped, and more Arm per device'.
Own-silicon step-change (2026-vintage thesis)
Artisan Developing World Fund, Tran Capital Midcap / Multi-Cap Growth, Baillie Gifford Positive Change, NZS Capital Growth Equity, SoftBank Group Corp.
Arm selling its own AGI CPU changes its economics. Tran models EPS rising from ~$2 (FY27) to $9+ (FY31); Artisan says it 'would allow the company to capture improved economics'. Baillie Gifford is the only one to probe the flip side: supply, gross margin and licensee reaction (paraphrased from its CFO meeting).
Sellers on valuation vs new buyers
Baillie Gifford Positive Change, SGA U.S. Large Cap Growth / Global Growth, Liontrust Global Technology / Global Innovation, NZS Capital Growth Equity, Hyperion Global Growth Companies Fund, Sands Capital Global Growth / Technology Innovators, Lansdowne Partners (UK) LLP, Altimeter Capital Management
Baillie Gifford ('market expectation is also now very high'), SGA, Liontrust, NZS and Hyperion (−67%, no reason given) cut in Q2 2026. Sands initiated in Q2, Lansdowne added +15% to a 15.6% 13F weight, and Altimeter opened in Q1 2026 — though Lansdowne and Altimeter publish no rationale.
Data-centre CPU vs edge/physical AI
Hyperion Global Growth Companies Fund, Sands Capital Global Growth / Technology Innovators, Liontrust Global Technology / Global Innovation
Most cards centre on hyperscaler CPUs. Hyperion (Orthman) instead puts the payoff at the edge ('cars ... humanoid robots'), over a 10–20 year horizon.
Controlling parent vs minority holders
SoftBank Group Corp.
SoftBank treats Arm as a strategic 'cornerstone' of its ASI platform and has borrowed ~$19.8B against Arm shares. Its agenda (Ampere, own silicon, the CEO's VCP pay plan) is set apart from minority economics — ISS opposed both the pay plan and Son's re-election (press, T3).

6. Research Questions

  1. Channel conflict: does selling the AGI CPU cost Arm licensing/royalty share with hyperscaler custom-CPU teams (Graviton, Axion, Cobalt)? Only Baillie Gifford raised it.
  2. Test Sands' claim that CPU royalties go from ~10% of revenue (2026) to >30% (2031) against the data-centre royalty disclosures in the FY2026 20-F and Q1 FY27 6-K.
  3. Stress-test Tran's EPS path (~$2 FY27 → $9+ FY31): what own-silicon revenue, gross margin and opex does it imply?
  4. Royalty rate per chip: SGA and Liontrust lean on v9/CSS rate uplift — is the blended rate still rising once own-silicon mix is included?
  5. SoftBank overhang: $19.8B non-recourse margin loan on pledged Arm shares plus ~13–14% free float — what happens to float and governance in a drawdown?
  6. Why did Hyperion cut 67% and Tensor Edge 47% in Q2 2026 with no public rationale, while Lansdowne added to a 15.6% weight?
  7. Governance: outcome of the 9-Sep-2026 AGM vote on Rene Haas's VCP and Son's re-election.
