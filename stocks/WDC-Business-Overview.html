1. Snapshot

Since distributing Sandisk on February 21, 2025, WDC makes only hard disk drives. Cloud customers bought 89% of FY2026 revenue of $12.9B, its top ten customers took 73%, and three alone took 16%, 15% and 13% (FY2026 10-K). Earnings are driven by cloud exabyte demand, a price per terabyte that is currently rising, and areal-density gains that lower cost per terabyte by roughly 8–10% a year. Cycle exposure is high: HDD revenue fell 31% in FY2023.

2. What the company does

Cloud operators must keep exabytes of video, backups, logs, data lakes and AI data online at the lowest cost per terabyte. WDC sells them that capacity, so the unit that matters is the terabyte, not the drive. Exabytes shipped went from 412 EB in FY2023 to 696 EB in FY2025 and an implied ~870 EB in FY2026 (FY2025 10-K; FY2026 10-K gives only +25%).

A terabyte starts in WDC's own fabs: it makes "substantially all" of its recording heads and magnetic media and buys controllers, preamps and DRAM, some from sole sources (FY2026 10-K). Drives are assembled mainly in Thailand, which holds $867M of WDC's $2.48B of long-lived assets, then qualified with each hyperscaler over several quarters. Volumes and prices are increasingly set in long-term agreements at "predetermined or formula-based prices", and customers pay within about two months.

In FY2023 the same terabyte sold for $15.18 but cost $11.81. By FY2026 it sold for $14.85 and cost $7.60 (inferred). Nearly all the profit improvement came from cost, which is why areal density, not price, is the long-run engine. Flash is gone because it carried heavy fab capex and its gross margin collapsed to 7.1% in FY2023 while HDD held 24.1% (FY2023 10-K). Client and Consumer drives, the PC-era lines most exposed to flash, are down to 11% of revenue.

3. Industry, competitive position & moat

HDDs survive because a platter still stores a terabyte far more cheaply than NAND. A 30TB QLC SSD cost about 4.9x a 30TB HDD per TB in Q2 2025 and 22.6x in Q1 2026, when NAND prices spiked (VDURA index via Blocks & Files). IDC estimates 89% of data at leading cloud providers sits on HDD. The ~5x pre-spike ratio is the better structural anchor (inferred).

The market has shrunk from more than 200 makers to three since 2013: Samsung's drives went to Seagate in 2011, Hitachi GST to WDC in 2012 for ~$4.8B, and regulators pushed some 3.5-inch assets to Toshiba. Units peaked at 651M in 2010 and were ~127M by 2023 while exabytes kept rising (Coughlin). The barriers that bind are in-house head and media nanofabrication, multi-quarter hyperscaler qualification, and the scale needed to fund density gains. Toshiba, which buys merchant components, lags a generation and holds ~11% of exabytes (inferred from Coughlin's 504 EB C2Q 2026 total).

The profit pool sits with the two integrated makers today, both above 44% non-GAAP operating margin in the June 2026 quarter. But US hyperscalers take ~60% of nearline shipments (TrendForce News), so buyer power returns when supply loosens.

WDC ships the most exabytes (~46% of the industry, inferred) at the higher gross margin, without HAMR. Its 40TB ePMR drives use 11 platters at ~3.6TB each; Seagate's HAMR Mozaic 4+ reaches 44TB at ~4.4TB per platter, and HAMR was ~40% of Seagate's nearline exabytes at FY2026 exit. More platters per terabyte means more media and head content per TB for WDC (inferred), a gap hidden while buyers take every drive available.

"At some point, the laws of physics will require us to transition to HAMR."WDC CEO Irving Tan, Innovation Day Q&A, February 2026 (Tom's Hardware). WDC guides 44TB HAMR for 1H CY2027.

Testing the company's claims: a consolidated, disciplined market is supported, but the same structure did not stop nearline exabytes falling ~45% in Q2 CY2023. A durable cost advantage over flash is supported. "Leadership in areal density and cost efficiency" is partially supported: WDC leads on margin and volume, while Seagate leads on density per platter. The maker that wins here turns density into the lowest cost per TB and is qualified first at the largest buyers. WDC is that company today; after 2027 it depends on a HAMR ramp not yet shown in volume.

4. Growth engine

Revenue equals exabytes times price per exabyte. FY2026 revenue rose 36% on +25% exabytes and +8% price (1.25 × 1.08 ≈ 1.35). With no acquisitions, reported growth is organic, but FY2026 had 53 weeks, worth ~2 points (inferred). The quarterly mix is moving from volume to price: Q3 grew 45% on +34% exabytes, Q4 grew 44% on +22% exabytes and "high teens" price per TB (web). Q1 FY2027 revenue is guided to $4.1B ± $0.1B.

1 · Cyclical on structural

Cloud exabyte demand. Cloud revenue +38% on +27% exabytes. The five largest US hyperscalers guided 2026 capex to $660–690B from ~$380B in 2025 (Futurum).

2 · Cyclical

Price per TB. Rising because both integrated makers refuse to add unit capacity during a demand surge; nearline contract prices +~10% in Q2 2026 (TrendForce). The first lever to reverse in a downturn.

3 · Structural

Areal density and mix. Each capacity step cuts cost per TB; 40TB ePMR to exceed 50% of nearline exabytes by Q3 FY2027, HAMR 44TB in 1H CY2027 and 50TB planned for 2H CY2027, if yields hold.

4 · Management-driven

Contract duration. LTAs run into 2029, with talks for 2030–2031. They buy visibility but cap upside at formula prices (FY2026 10-K).

5 · Temporary

Client and Consumer. Client price per EB +26% on flash shortages; structurally shrinking lines now 11% of revenue.

5. Margin, cash & capital allocation

Gross margin went from 22.2% in FY2023 to 48.9% in FY2026 and 54.4% non-GAAP in Q4. The mechanism is operating leverage on a vertically integrated, largely fixed cost base: idle head and media fabs cost ~$200M of unabsorbed overhead in FY2023 and ~$155M in FY2024, and now they run full while each new capacity point lowers cost per TB. Management cited incremental gross margins of ~84–85% in Q4 (web). R&D fell to 9.0% and SG&A to 4.3% of revenue, and capex was only $418M (3.2%, against a 4–6% guide that rises in FY2027). Free cash flow was ~$3.5B (inferred).

The retained 19.9% Sandisk stake was never sold for cash. It was swapped tax-free to retire term loans and a bridge loan and to buy back 4.8M WDC shares. Even so, shares outstanding rose from 347M to 361M because a convertible exchange cost $860M cash plus 21.3M shares and the preferred converted into 7M shares. This follows a decade spent repaying the $17.5B SanDisk debt, with the dividend suspended from 2020 to 2025. No transaction is pending.

6. Cyclicality, constraints & what to monitor

When hyperscalers pause, they stop buying capacity and burn inventory. In FY2023 WDC's HDD exabytes fell ~26% and revenue 31%, while Seagate's non-GAAP gross margin hit 19.5% in its trough quarter. WDC now says variability is "much less than in the past"; LTAs support that, but none has been tested in a downturn, and their remedies offset only "at least some portion" of lost revenue.

The business is at the top of its own history: 54.4% gross margin against a 22.2% trough and a 29.4% prior HDD peak, with price rising and supply sold out. The downside is concrete: a capex digestion after 2026 combined with NAND supply recovery would cut price per TB first, idle the fabs next, and pull margin back toward the 20s. Supply discipline is softer than it looks because merchant suppliers are expanding (Resonac media +31%, a new Hoya substrate plant in 2028). China's rare-earth export restrictions and an expired Thailand tax holiday add cost pressure.

7. Risks, unknowns & questions

  • HAMR execution. Seagate targets 70% of nearline exabytes on HAMR by June 2027 (one call-slide summary). If WDC's ramp slips, its 11-platter drives carry more cost per TB just as supply loosens, costing share and price at the same customers.
  • Concentration meets contract terms. Three customers are 44% of revenue and two held 42% of receivables. LTAs cap upside at formula prices, while one buyer moving a tier to QLC or toward Seagate removes a sixth of revenue.
  • China and export controls. China 19.7% and Hong Kong 11.5% of ship-to revenue plus a Shenzhen site; Seagate's $300M BIS settlement over Huawei is the precedent.
  • Discipline eroded from below. Component suppliers adding capacity make unit additions easier for any of the three makers.
  • Dilution offsets buybacks. Convertible premiums are settled in shares; 21.3M were issued in June 2026.
  • Separation tax. Loss of tax-free status on the spin or share exchanges could create "significant tax liabilities".

Not answered by the sources: absolute FY2026 exabytes, drive units and capacity per drive (the FY2026 10-K dropped them); LTA pricing formulas and enforceability; HAMR cost and yield; 2026 vendor shares by exabyte; customer identities and true Chinese end-demand; maintenance versus growth capex; Toshiba's HDD profitability; a $22M unexplained FY2023 net-loss difference between filings.

8. Investor takeaways

  • What it is: a pure-play, vertically integrated HDD maker selling bulk cloud capacity in a three-player market.
  • Engine: price per TB minus a cost per TB falling ~8–10% a year; gross profit per TB ~$3.37 → ~$7.25 from FY2023 to FY2026.
  • Growth lever: cloud exabytes growing 25%+ under multi-year agreements.
  • What breaks it: a capex pause plus a lagging HAMR ramp and cheaper NAND, handing pricing power back to three customers who are 44% of revenue.
  • Monitor: price per TB, HAMR volume in 1H CY2027 versus Seagate, gross margin versus the FY2023 trough.
