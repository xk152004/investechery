The Call
S&P Global is the stronger business for the next 5 to 10 years: it owns three benchmark franchises where Moody's owns one, and runs both of the businesses they share at a higher margin.
The one load-bearing fact: replaying the 2022 issuance collapse on FY2025 mix would cut about 16% of S&P Global's segment profit but about 27% of Moody's, because ratings are 48% of S&P's profit and 70% of Moody's (inferred from FY2025 segment data and the 2022 decline in both 10-Ks).
1
Ratings is a shared tide, not a contest. The two agencies grew US ratings revenue at 22% a year from FY2023 to FY2025 and both lost about 90 cents of profit per dollar of revenue in 2022 (FY2023 and FY2025 10-Ks, both). Neither wins the ratings cell on growth; S&P wins it on margin and on a larger annuity share.
2
S&P Global has two more benchmarks Moody's cannot build: S&P Dow Jones Indices ($1,850m revenue, 69% margin, +14% in FY2025) and Platts price assessments inside Energy ($2,299m, 41% margin) (SPGI FY2025 10-K).
3
Moody's wins one cell outright: its analytics arm grows ARR 8 to 9% with 95% retention against about 6% ACV growth at S&P's Market Intelligence (MCO Q2 2026 call; SPGI Q2 2026 call). That is Moody's route to closing the gap, and the thing to watch.

The Three Answers

1. Who has the stronger growth profile, by product x geography?
Moody's, narrowly, on rate; S&P Global on breadth

Three cells carry the next five years. First, ratings in both regions, where growth comes from refinancing walls and new borrowers: S&P expects about $11 trillion of rated debt to refinance over the next four and a half years, and AI infrastructure is adding hyperscaler, data-centre and power issuance (S&P guides $250 to 300bn of hyperscaler issuance for 2026) (SPGI Q2 2026 call). Both agencies ride that equally, but Moody's gets more of it per dollar of revenue because 67% of its ratings revenue is transaction-based against 52% at S&P (FY2025 10-Ks). Second, the data and workflow cell, where Moody's Analytics grows ARR 8 to 9% on KYC (+13%), lending and insurance cloud migration, while S&P's Market Intelligence platforms (Capital IQ and similar) grow low single digits and only its Kensho data feeds grow high single to low double digits (Q2 2026 calls, both). Third, equity indices, where S&P alone collects asset-linked fees on $6.35tn of ETF assets that grow with markets and the shift to passive (SPGI Q2 2026 call). Weighting each cell's run-rate by its revenue share gives roughly 7.7% a year for Moody's against 7.2% for S&P ex Mobility (inferred), a gap inside the noise; S&P's 2026 organic guide is 6 to 8% (SPGI Q2 2026 call).

2. Who converts that growth into superior margins?
S&P Global, narrowly, and on better quality

The consolidated statements point the wrong way: Moody's shows a 72.8% gross-margin proxy against 68.8% at S&P (3-year averages, 10-Ks). The segment data reverses it. On a like basis S&P Ratings earned 63.8% in FY2025 against 61.6% at Moody's MIS, and S&P led in every year since FY2023, though the lead has narrowed from 3.9 to 2.2 points (inferred basis: MIS adjusted operating income less its D&A). Market Intelligence earns about 32% before acquisition amortization against about 28% for Moody's Analytics on a comparable basis (MCO figure inferred). Mix then adds Indices at 69% and Energy at 41% GAAP. Cash confirms it: free cash flow was 35.6% of revenue at S&P and 33.4% at Moody's in FY2025, partly because Moody's capitalizes more software (capex 4.2% of revenue against 1.3%) (FY2025 10-Ks). The tension: Moody's grows a little faster, S&P keeps a little more of each dollar. S&P is the fortress with a slower data arm; Moody's is the more levered ratings compounder with a faster but thinner analytics arm.

3. Where do the vulnerabilities sit if the tide turns?
Moody's breaks first

Moody's single exposure is the issuance cycle: ratings are 53% of revenue and about 70% of adjusted operating income, and two-thirds of ratings revenue is transaction fees (FY2025 10-K). In 2022 MIS revenue fell 28% and adjusted operating income fell 40%, about 89 cents of profit per lost dollar (FY2023 10-K). S&P's ratings behaved the same way (revenue -26%, profit -36%), but ratings are now only 48% of S&P's segment profit and 52% transaction. Replaying 2022 on FY2025 mix costs Moody's about 27% of adjusted operating income and S&P about 16% of segment profit (inferred). S&P's own weak points are different: a bear market hits Indices asset-linked fees ($1,206m) almost one for one, and its balance sheet is now more stretched, with gross leverage guided to 2.8x at end-2026, above its 2.0 to 2.5x target, to fund more than $7bn of 2026 buybacks (SPGI Q2 2026 call). Moody's carried $6.9bn of debt against $3.2bn of equity at 30 June 2026 (MCO Q2 2026 10-Q). In an issuance downturn Moody's breaks first; in an equity bear market combined with tighter credit, S&P's leverage leaves less room.

Segment growth engines

Ratings. Growth is issuance volume times price. Both disclose the same drivers for FY2025: tight spreads, refinancing, AI-related investment-grade issuance, CLOs and private credit (S&P private-market ratings +60% in Q2 2026; Moody's private-credit transactions +40%) (Q2 2026 calls). S&P's billed issuance was $4.3tn in FY2025 (+11%); Moody's rated more than $2tn in each of Q1 and Q2 2026. Price shows up in the annuity line at both: Moody's recurring revenue rose on "annual price increases and higher monitored credits", S&P's on "improved contract terms" (FY2025 10-Ks). Ratings growth is essentially organic at both.

Data and workflow. Moody's MA grows through embedding in regulated workflows: KYC ARR +15% in FY2025, insurance cat modelling migrating to its cloud platform with less than half of customers moved, and lending suites repriced on renewal (MCO FY2025 10-K; Q2 2026 call). S&P's MI is being split into a fast Kensho data business (about a third of the Kensho Data and Platforms line, growing high single to low double digits) and slower platforms (low single digit) to be consolidated for margin (SPGI Q2 2026 call). Both are partly bought: S&P added Visible Alpha (2024) and With Intelligence (2025); Moody's spent $227m on acquisitions in FY2025 and sold its Learning Solutions business in Q4 2025 (MCO FY2025 10-K).

Indices. Asset-linked fees rose 22% in FY2024 and 15% in FY2025 as average ETF assets grew 33% and 24% (SPGI FY2025 10-K). Market levels do most of the work; net inflows of more than $600bn over the last year and asset-manager switches to S&P add the structural part (SPGI Q2 2026 call).

Insight: S&P's fastest-growing cell (Indices, +15% a year) is also its highest-margin cell; Moody's fastest-growing cell (KYC and Decision Solutions) sits in its lowest-margin segment. Implication: Each point of growth is worth more profit at S&P than at Moody's. KPI: S&P asset-linked fees / average ETF AUM, 2.73bps in FY2024 and 2.53bps in FY2025 (inferred); a fall below 2.3bps signals fee compression. [Source: SPGI FY2025 10-K, Indices MD&A]

Price control and route-to-market

Both sell direct. In ratings the issuer pays and has no substitute for the big-agency ratings that index and mandate rules require, so both exercise annual price increases. The difference is how price is packaged: 48% of S&P Ratings revenue is non-transaction (surveillance, annual relationship fees, Crisil) against 33% recurring at Moody's (FY2025 10-Ks). Moody's also notes that frequent issuers on pricing programs carry lower average revenue yields, so higher volume from banks and data-centre issuers did not lift its 2026 revenue guide (MCO Q2 2026 call). In indices S&P prices by basis points on assets, a meter that runs with markets rather than with negotiations.

Insight: S&P has converted more of its ratings pricing into annuity fees. Implication: Its ratings revenue falls less in a drought and its net price is less dependent on deal flow. KPI: MIS recurring revenue share of ratings revenue (33% FY2025); above 38% by FY2028 would close the gap. [Source: FY2025 10-Ks]

Supply resilience

Neither company has physical supply risk; the inputs are analysts, engineers, data and cloud capacity. Compensation is the largest cost at Moody's ($2,574m of $3,776m operating and SG&A) (MCO FY2025 10-K); S&P does not split its cost base this way at group level. S&P's one partner dependency is CME Group, which co-owns S&P Dow Jones Indices and holds a put on at least 20% of its stake ($4.9bn redeemable non-controlling interest); Moody's depends on AWS and Microsoft as distribution partners for its AI and cloud products (SPGI FY2025 10-K; MCO Q2 2026 call).

Risks by segment

Ratings: an issuance drought hits both, but Moody's more (see Answer 3). Data: AI tools that let clients combine feeds from several vendors threaten stand-alone platforms first, which is S&P's slow-growing Capital IQ-type estate more than Moody's workflow-embedded KYC and insurance software; an analyst raised exactly this on S&P's Q2 2026 call. Indices: a bear market cuts asset-linked fees directly, and switching by large asset managers works in both directions. Energy: geopolitics; S&P chose to be flexible on price with customers affected by the Iran conflict in 2026 (SPGI Q2 2026 call).

On cells alone Moody's has the slightly faster engine, because its data arm outgrows S&P's; S&P has the broader one, because two of its cells have no Moody's counterpart.
In this industry the power sits with whoever other people's rules point to: index eligibility rules, investment mandates, futures contracts and ETF prospectuses.

Moats: what rivals cannot copy

S&P Global

Ratings: named in bond-index and mandate rules alongside Moody's and Fitch; the SEC staff report identifies these guidelines, index rules and the QIB certification requirement as barriers to entry. Durability High. Indices: the S&P 500 underlies the first $1tn ETF, four of the five ETFs the US Treasury selected for Thrift accounts and SPX derivatives; replacing it means rebuilding a liquid market. Durability High. Platts: price assessments settle exchange contracts. Durability High, with 2026 geopolitical noise.

Moody's

Ratings: the same mandate-driven moat, with an identical 2022 to 2025 path. Durability High. Analytics: Orbis ownership data on more than 630m entities, cat models for insurers, credit models embedded in bank lending workflows, 95% retention. Durability Medium: valuable and sticky, but money and time can buy a competitor; margins in the low 30s are the evidence the market does not treat it as a benchmark.

Insight: Both own the ratings moat equally; only S&P owns two further references (the S&P 500 family and Platts) that third parties have written into products and contracts. Implication: S&P's profit pool has three independent protections against one at Moody's; Moody's second business is protected by switching costs, not by mandates. KPI: Net ETF flows captured by S&P DJI indices and asset-manager switches disclosed each quarter; any large switch away is the first crack. [Source: SEC OCR 2025 Staff Report pp.18, 29-30; SPGI Q2 2026 call; MCO Q2 2026 call]

Customers: who controls net price and access

S&P: no customer above 10% of revenue; in ratings 48% of revenue is non-transaction; in indices it is paid by asset managers on their assets and by exchanges on volume, and management reports asset managers switching to S&P benchmarks in Q2 2026 (SPGI FY2025 10-K; Q2 2026 call). Moody's: no customer above 10% of receivables; ratings recurring share 33%; MA 96% to 99% recurring with 95% retention and upsell on platform migrations, but lower-yield pricing programs for frequent issuers dilute ratings price per dollar issued (MCO FY2025 10-K; Q2 2026 call). Both raise ratings prices annually; neither discloses the price contribution.

Insight: S&P has turned more of its issuer relationships into annual fees and is paid by asset-based meters in indices; Moody's has the stickier software customer base (95% retention). Implication: In a downturn S&P's net price holds better in ratings; in data Moody's holds customers better. KPI: MIS recurring share (33%) vs S&P non-transaction share (48%); MA retention stays at or above 94%. [Source: FY2025 10-Ks; MCO Q2 2026 call]

Suppliers: who absorbs shocks

S&P: people and data; low capital intensity (capex 1.3% of revenue); CME partnership in indices. Moody's: people and cloud; capex 4.2% of revenue as it capitalizes software for MA's cloud shift, and it is sunsetting on-premise insurance models, which makes AWS a critical platform (FY2025 10-Ks; MCO Q2 2026 call). Pass-through: both held or raised margins through the 2022 to 2023 wage inflation once issuance recovered; neither shows input-cost stress.

Insight: Neither has a supplier that can dictate terms; S&P's lower capital intensity and Moody's cloud migration are the only real differences. Implication: Moody's carries more execution risk in moving insurance customers to its cloud platform on AWS. KPI: Moody's capex as % of revenue falling below 3.5% once migration peaks; S&P capex stays under 2%. [Source: FY2025 10-Ks, cash flow statements; MCO Q2 2026 call]

The causal gap

1. The index franchise (Major). S&P: $1,850m, 69% margin; Moody's: none. Not closable: it would take decades and a liquid derivatives market. 2. Ratings annuity mix (Moderate). S&P 48% non-transaction vs Moody's 33% recurring. Partly closable over 3 to 5 years through relationship pricing and first-time mandates (Moody's expects 750 to 850 in 2026), but it is also a legacy of how each agency priced for decades. 3. Data arm economics (Moderate, and moving toward Moody's). MI is larger and more profitable; MA grows faster and targets mid to high 30s margins by end-2027 (MCO Q2 2026 call). If MA gets there while holding 9% ARR growth, this cause flips to Moody's within about two years.

S&P Global holds the stronger power position because it owns three references others must use; Moody's owns one, plus a good software business that must still earn its margins.

The structural gap

The most persistent gap is the operating-expense ratio: Moody's ran 3 to 5 points lower in each of the three years. It is not a cost-discipline story. It comes from mix, since Moody's is 53% ratings while S&P's cost base carried Energy, Mobility and Market Intelligence content operations, and from accounting, since Moody's capitalizes about three times as much of its revenue as capex, moving software cost from operating expense into amortization (inferred). At segment level the gap reverses: S&P Ratings out-earns MIS by 2 to 4 points every year and MI out-earns MA by about 4 points on a comparable basis. That confirms the power map: the company with more benchmark pricing power shows it where the businesses overlap, and in free cash flow (35.6% vs 33.4%). Moody's lead in consolidated gross margin is a mix effect a reader should not mistake for a cheaper engine.

S&P Global runs the leaner engine segment for segment; Moody's looks leaner only because of what it does not own and what it capitalizes.

AI lens: Market Intelligence vs Moody's Analytics

The question is revenue at risk against revenue gained, bucket by bucket, not the headline AI growth rates. AI hurts seat-based screens and analyst research, because an AI front end can pull the same answer from several vendors. It helps data sold as a licence to feed a customer's own models. Usage counts only matter if they show up in the data-licence bucket. If S&P's API calls keep compounding while Kensho Data growth stays in the high single digits and Platforms slows, the AI traffic is substituting for seats. The two disclosures that would turn this lens from qualitative to quantitative are each company's AI-cohort share of recurring revenue and S&P's MI net retention.

What would flip the call

  • Moody's Analytics becomes a second benchmark-quality business: ARR growth holds at 9% or better while adjusted margin reaches the mid to high 30s by end-2027, making Moody's the faster grower with comparable conversion. Early warning: MA adjusted margin at or above 36% with ARR growth at or above 9% in FY2027 (MCO 10-K).
  • AI hollows out S&P's Market Intelligence platforms, the 36% of revenue ex Mobility that grows slowest and, per the AI lens above, carries the larger unmeasured exposed bucket: clients use AI front ends to mix feeds from several vendors and consolidate away from Capital IQ-type platforms. Early warning: MI organic growth below 4% or ACV growth below 5% for two consecutive quarters (SPGI earnings calls).
