The Three Answers

1. Who has the stronger growth profile, by product x geography?
AMD

AMD, clearly. Three cells carry its next five years. In AI accelerators, AMD holds multi-year agreements for up to 6GW with OpenAI (Oct 2025), up to 6GW with Meta (8-K, 24 Feb 2026) and up to 2GW with Anthropic (AMD release, 22 Jul 2026), with MI450 and Helios racks starting to ship in Q3 2026 and stepping up into 2027, so the 2027 ramp is contracted rather than hoped for. In server CPUs, AMD reached 46.2% of x86 server revenue in Q1 2026 (Mercury Research via Tom's Hardware, approx., unverified) and guides server CPU revenue up more than 70% in 2027 (Q2 FY26 call, approx., unverified), because each EPYC generation ships on a TSMC node Intel has only just matched. In client PCs, units rose 34% in Q2 2026 (10-Q). Intel's 2026 recovery is real but different in kind: Q2 client revenue rose on ASPs up 27% while volume fell 8%, and server ASPs rose 48% on volume up 9%, with demand exceeding supply (Q2 FY26 10-Q). Price-led growth in a shortage fades when supply catches up; share and new categories do not. Geographically the mix is similar (China 24% of Intel revenue, 22% of AMD in FY25), but Intel's China billings fell 18% in 2025 while AMD's rose 24% (10-Ks).

Revenue indexed to 100 in FY2021 (Intel $79.0B, AMD $16.4B). LTM Q2 2026 = FY25 less 1H25 plus 1H26: Intel $57.0B, AMD $41.3B. AMD 2022 includes Xilinx from Feb 2022 (acquired, not organic). Sources: FY23-FY25 10-Ks, Q2 FY26 10-Qs.
2. Who converts that growth into superior margins?
AMD

AMD at the company level, through a variable cost base: it pays TSMC only for wafers it buys, so higher Data Center mix flows to gross margin (46.1% in 2023, 49.5% in 2025, 53.3% in 1H26, GAAP; 56% non-GAAP guided for Q3 2026). Intel's consolidated margin is held down by manufacturing: Intel Foundry lost $10.3B in 2025 and $4.5B in 1H26 while its 18A ramp adds higher-cost wafers (10-K, 10-Q). The tension worth stating plainly: Intel's design business now out-earns AMD's segments. Intel Products ran a 32% operating margin in 1H26 (DCAI 40% in Q2) against AMD Data Center at 30% and Client and Gaming at 16% (10-Qs, segment basis, both excluding stock comp and acquisition amortization). Intel has a fortress product franchise attached to a loss-making factory; AMD has an average-margin but compounding engine. AMD's own caution: early MI450 revenue will sit slightly below the corporate gross margin (Q2 FY26 call, approx., unverified).

GAAP, % of revenue. Intel 1H26 EBIT includes a $3.9B goodwill impairment (9.8% excluding restructuring and other charges). Sources: FY23-FY25 10-Ks; Q2 FY26 10-Qs.
3. Where do the vulnerabilities sit if the tide turns?
Intel breaks first

Intel's exposure is cost rigidity plus capital need. Depreciation was $10.8B in 2025, 2026 capex is guided above $20B, and 14A economics depend on landing outside customers (10-K; Q2 FY26 call). In 2024 a 2% revenue decline produced an $11.7B operating loss after $3.3B of fab impairments (FY24 and FY25 10-K). A significant portion of revenue also comes from Intel 7 products made in its Israel fab, which Intel self-insures against war (Q2 FY26 10-Q). AMD's exposure is concentration: Data Center is 58% of revenue, AI GPU volume is anchored by a handful of labs that were paid in warrants for up to 320M AMD shares (about 20% of 1,632M outstanding; 8-K 24 Feb 2026, 10-Q), and every leading-edge wafer comes from TSMC in Taiwan. In a general downturn Intel breaks first, because its costs do not flex; AMD breaks first only in an AI-capex pause or a Taiwan disruption.

Segment growth engines

Server CPU. AMD's driver is product cadence on TSMC's leading nodes and a platform its customers already qualified; Intel's is the Xeon installed base and, from 2026, 18A products. AMD's server revenue share climbed to 46.2% in Q1 2026 while its unit share was 34.5% in Q2 (Mercury Research via press, approx., unverified), so AMD sells the higher-priced parts. Intel's 2025 server ASP fell 4% on pricing actions taken in a competitive environment (FY25 10-K); its 2026 ASP gains come with demand above supply.

AI accelerators. AMD's growth here is mostly organic: ZT Systems (acquired Mar 2025, $4.4B) was kept for rack design and its manufacturing arm was sold to Sanmina for $2.4B (FY25 10-K). Intel has no product in volume: Gaudi produced $922M of inventory charges in 2024 and Crescent Island and Jaguar Shores are in development (FY25 10-K).

Client. Intel's 18A Panther Lake (Core Ultra Series 3) entered volume in early 2026 and is the first product on its own leading node since the delays of 2018-2023; AMD's gains come from Ryzen mobile. The client market itself is under pressure from memory prices (Mercury via Tom's Hardware reports desktop shipments down 20%, approx., unverified).

Foundry. Intel committed to finishing 14A in Q2 2026 (10-Q), with risk production targeted for 2H 2027 and volume in 2028 (Q2 FY26 call, approx., unverified). A preliminary Apple-Intel manufacturing agreement was reported by the Wall Street Journal on 8 May 2026 but Intel has not confirmed a named 14A customer in its filings (TheStreet citing WSJ, approx., unverified).

Price control and route-to-market

Intel sells mostly through PC and server OEMs: its three largest customers were 43% of 2025 revenue (19%, 12%, 12%) and it paid incentives in 1H24 that pulled volume forward (FY25 10-K). AMD had no 10% customer in 2024 or 2025, but in AI GPUs it bought access with equity: performance warrants for up to 160M shares each to OpenAI and Meta at a $0.01 strike (8-K 24 Feb 2026; FY25 10-K). Right now Intel holds price better in client (ASP +27% vs AMD -6% in Q2) because Intel is supply-short and AMD is taking units with lower-priced mobile parts.

Insight: Intel sets client price only while the market is short; AMD pays equity for AI GPU volume. Implication: Neither has clean pricing power: Intel's depends on the cycle, AMD's AI growth carries a dilution cost that scales with success. KPI: Intel client ASP YoY after supply normalizes (10-Q); AMD warrant tranche vesting (10-Q equity note). [Source: Intel FY25 10-K and Q2 FY26 10-Q; AMD 8-K 24 Feb 2026 and Q2 FY26 10-Q.]

Supply resilience

Intel makes most of its products in its own fabs but uses TSMC for key tiles, depends on ASML as sole EUV supplier and reports industry shortages of substrates and memory that it expects to persist into 2027 (FY25 10-K; Q2 FY26 10-Q). AMD uses TSMC for all wafers at 7nm and below, GlobalFoundries for older nodes, and third parties in China, Malaysia and Taiwan for assembly and test, with $12.2B of purchase commitments at end-2025 and supply prepayments in 1H26 (FY25 10-K; Q2 FY26 10-Q). Intel controls more of its supply but carries its fixed cost; AMD is flexible but single-sourced at the leading edge.

Risks by segment

Server CPU: Intel loses more share if 18A Xeons slip, AMD if TSMC allocates scarce capacity toward Nvidia or Apple. AI accelerators: AMD alone is exposed, to an AI capex pause and to export rules (a $800M MI308 charge in 2025, partly reversed; FY25 10-K). Client: both face Arm PCs and memory-driven unit weakness, Intel more because it has more to lose. China: 24% of Intel revenue, falling, vs 22% of AMD, rising (FY25 10-Ks).

AMD's growth comes from share and new categories under contract; Intel's comes from price in a shortage. The first compounds, the second mean-reverts.
In CPUs the terms are set by whoever ships the better part on the better node first; manufacturing ownership has mattered less than design cadence for eight years.

Moats: what rivals cannot copy

Intel

x86 installed base: Xeon is 'a significant portion of global server infrastructure' (FY25 10-K). Durability Med: AMD took server revenue share to ~46% on the same instruction set, so the base is portable. US leading-edge manufacturing: the only company doing leading-edge logic R&D and volume production in the US (FY25 10-K). Durability High to replicate, but not yet economic. Policy backing: US government holds 275M shares plus escrow and CHIPS funds were accelerated ($5.7B, 8-K 29 Aug 2025); Nvidia invested $5B and co-develops x86 products. Durability Med.

AMD

Design cadence on TSMC's leading nodes with chiplets: proof is share gains in server and client every year (Mercury, approx., unverified). Durability Med-High. Full-stack AI (CPU, GPU, NIC, rack design from ZT, ROCm software): proof is 14GW of multi-year agreements. Durability Med, since ROCm still trails CUDA and access was partly paid in warrants. FPGA and adaptive SoCs from Xilinx (AMD targets >70% share, Analyst Day 11 Nov 2025). Durability High.

Insight: Intel's hardest-to-copy asset is its fabs, which currently destroy value; AMD's advantage is execution, which is copyable in principle but has compounded for eight years. Implication: The moat race turns on whether Intel's manufacturing becomes an advantage (18A/14A at cost parity) before AMD's execution lead widens further. KPI: Intel Foundry operating loss per quarter, and Mercury server revenue share. [Source: Intel FY25 10-K; Q2 FY26 10-Qs; Mercury via press.]

Customers: who controls net price and access

Intel: OEM-concentrated (top three customers 43% of 2025 revenue) and a price-taker in 2024-25, then a price-setter in the 2026 shortage. AMD: no 10% customer in 2024-25, broad hyperscaler base for EPYC, but AI GPU demand anchored by OpenAI, Meta and Anthropic, with warrants to the first two worth up to about 20% of shares outstanding. In a softening market, pressure lands on Intel in client and server CPUs (OEMs shift share of wallet) and on AMD in AI GPUs (a few buyers set the pace).

Insight: AMD has the broader CPU customer base; Intel has the stronger client channel today. Implication: AMD wins on breadth; its AI concentration is the offset. KPI: Share of AMD Data Center revenue from its largest AI customers (not disclosed; watch for a 10% customer disclosure in the FY26 10-K). [Source: 10-Ks; 8-Ks.]

Suppliers: who absorbs shocks

Intel: vertically integrated, but TSMC for tiles, ASML sole EUV source, substrate and memory shortages, helium shortage from the Iran conflict, and an uninsured Intel 7 fab in Israel. Dual-sourcing Med; pass-through Partial (2026 pricing partly offsets input costs; gross margin ~40%). AMD: TSMC single source at 7nm and below, no long-term commitments with some suppliers. Dual-sourcing Low; pass-through Strong in this cycle (gross margin up from 46% to 53% through 2023-1H26).

Insight: Intel owns more of its supply chain; AMD has passed costs through better. Implication: Intel wins continuity in a Taiwan disruption; AMD wins on cost flexibility in every other scenario. KPI: Intel gross margin vs guidance 'comfortably in the 40s' (Q2 FY26 call); AMD non-GAAP gross margin vs 56% guide for Q3 2026. [Source: 10-Ks; Q2 FY26 10-Qs; calls.]

The causal gap

Three causes explain most of the difference. (1) Manufacturing model, Major: Intel carries $10.8B of annual depreciation and $3.3B of 2024 impairments; closing it needs external foundry volume on 18A and 14A, a 3-5 year task. (2) Node access and execution 2018-2024, Major: AMD shipped on TSMC's best nodes while Intel slipped; 18A in volume in 2026 narrows this in 2-3 years if 14A follows on time. (3) AI accelerator portfolio, Major: AMD has an annual MI roadmap and rack systems, Intel has none in volume; this is not money-buyable in under three years.

AMD holds the stronger power position, narrowly: its advantage is execution, Intel's is manufacturing that is strategic but not yet economic.
AMD runs leaner, and the whole gap is in cost of sales: Intel's factories, not its product teams.

Both report by function under US GAAP, so no by-nature mapping was needed. COGS is total cost of sales (AMD's includes about $1B a year of acquired-intangible amortization; Intel's includes fab depreciation, impairments and under-utilization). SG&A is marketing, general and administrative. Restructuring (Intel) and operating amortization (AMD) sit outside the five ratios and bridge to EBIT. The structural gap

Gross margin is the persistent structural gap: 12.5 points on the three-year average and 13.4 points in 1H26, present in every period. The mechanism is the manufacturing model from the power map. Intel's depreciation rose from $7.8B to $10.8B between 2023 and 2025 and sits in cost of sales, and 2024 added $3.3B of fab impairments; AMD pays TSMC a margin but only on wafers it buys. The cross-check confirms the power map rather than contradicting it: Intel's product segments earn more than AMD's (Intel Products 32% operating margin in 1H26), so the gap is not weak pricing, it is the factory. That makes the gap closable in principle, by filling fabs with outside customers, but not by cost-cutting: Intel's combined R&D and SG&A were already down 7% in 1H26 after two restructurings. The one line where Intel is leaner, SG&A, reflects those cuts and AMD's go-to-market build-out.

AMD converts each dollar of sales into about 13 more cents of gross profit; closing that gap requires Intel Foundry customers, not Intel cost cuts.

What would flip the call

  • Intel lands a definitive 14A anchor customer and Intel Foundry's loss shrinks toward breakeven by 2027, turning the fab base from a cost into a second profit engine with scarce US capacity. Early warning: Intel Foundry quarterly operating loss below $1.0B by Q4 2027 and a named 14A customer in an 8-K or 10-Q (Intel filings).
  • AMD's MI450 and Helios ramp slips or yields stay low, so 2027 AI growth falls short and the warrant-linked anchor customers delay purchases. Early warning: AMD Data Center AI revenue growth in 2027 below 'well over 100%'; first warrant tranche (1GW shipped) not vesting by mid-2027 (AMD 10-Q equity note).
  • Intel's 18A Xeons restore performance leadership and AMD's server share stalls below its >50% revenue-share target. Early warning: Mercury x86 server revenue share: AMD flat or down for two consecutive quarters in 2027 (Mercury Research, via press).

Where to spend your time
Spend the next hours on AMD's Data Center unit economics (MI450 margins, customer concentration, warrant dilution), because that is where the stronger business can still disappoint. For Intel, only one piece of work moves the call: the Intel Foundry P&L path to breakeven and the 14A customer book.
