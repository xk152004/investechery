Business Overview

Alphabet Inc. (NASDAQ: GOOG / GOOGL)

Alphabet is an advertising auction of extraordinary quality being used to finance the construction of an AI infrastructure business — and as of 2026 the construction, not the auction, is what determines the reported numbers.
The economic engine
Two units: one paid click, and one contracted accelerator-hour.

Nothing is billed until a user clicks an ad. On the click, the advertiser pays the auction-clearing price and Google pays a share away as traffic acquisition cost. In Q2 2026 that cost was 19.8% of Google advertising revenue — so roughly 80c of every advertising dollar is retained, and Google Services converted 41.8% of its revenue into operating income. The second unit is sold before it is delivered: $513.9bn of Google Cloud revenue backlog sat under contract at 30 June 2026, about 6.6 times the segment's last twelve months of revenue, converting at a 35.6% margin.

2 · What the company does

Alphabet solves two unrelated problems for two unrelated customers. For an advertiser, the problem is that most advertising is bought before anyone has expressed a want; Google sells the moment after the want is stated. For an enterprise, the problem is that training and serving AI models requires accelerators, power and floor space almost nobody can procure at scale; Google Cloud rents that stack out. The two businesses share an engineering base and almost nothing else economically.

Tracing one unit

An advertiser sets bids in an auction. A user types a query. Google ranks ads against it and serves them. Nothing is billed until the click. On the click the advertiser is charged the clearing price, and Google pays a share away — to the browser, handset maker or carrier that supplied the query, or to the third-party publisher that supplied the page. What remains converts at a very high rate, because the marginal cost of ranking one more query is close to nothing.

In Q2 2026 Google advertising revenue was $81,629m against $16,179m of traffic acquisition cost, a rate of 19.8%. Alphabet discloses only percentage changes in clicks and price, never absolute counts, so revenue per click cannot be computed from the filings and no figure claiming to do so should be trusted.

Search & other is owned inventory — Google controls the surface, the auction and the user relationship. Google Network is the opposite: ads sold on other people's sites through AdSense, AdMob and Ad Manager, carrying a far higher traffic acquisition cost because most of the money is paid straight to the publisher. Network has now declined for sixteen consecutive quarters, from a $9,305m peak in Q4 2021 to $7,303m in Q2 2026, while group revenue roughly doubled.

That decline is the most informative line in the disclosure. The shrinking part of the business is the part where Alphabet is an intermediary rather than a principal, and it is the low-margin part. Some of the Services margin expansion of the last three years is simply mix: the worse business got smaller.

A third unit, barely visible yet

The Q2 2026 10-Q rewrote Google Cloud's revenue policy to name "the sale of TPU systems" as the source of product revenue, and inventory rose from $2,439m at December 2025 to $9,991m at June 2026, $6.7bn of that in Q2 alone. Management said delivery to customer data centres began in the quarter and that most of the revenue lands in 2027. Alphabet is now building accelerators to sell, not only to run.

3 · Industry, competitive position & moat

Global advertising revenue excluding US political spend is forecast to grow 8.9% in 2026 to $1.3 trillion, with the US up 11.9% (WPP Media, midyear forecast, June 2026). Dentsu's December 2025 forecast put growth at 5.1% on a narrower definition; the gap is definitional rather than a dispute about direction. Profit in this industry accrues to whoever owns the surface where purchase intent is expressed, not to the intermediaries who move budget across surfaces — which is exactly what Alphabet's own segment margins demonstrate.

Barriers to entry here are routinely misdescribed. Advertiser switching costs are close to zero: a media buyer can move budget between Google, Meta and Amazon within a day. The barrier that binds is on the user side — a click-relevance feedback loop trained on more than five trillion queries a year, coupled with owned distribution through Android and Chrome.

On scale Alphabet is unmatched: roughly $315bn of last-twelve-months advertising revenue exceeds Meta's $201bn and Amazon's $68.6bn combined. But Amazon advertising grew 22.1% and Meta 22.2% against 17.9% for Alphabet's Search & other line in H1 2026. Alphabet is losing share of a growing advertising pool while gaining share of cloud. The growth story has migrated from ads to infrastructure.

Google Cloud is 60% of AWS's size and growing roughly 3.7 times as fast, with the margin gap to AWS now about four points and closing. That is the clearest competitive fact available, and it is measured against competitors' own reported figures rather than the company's characterisation of itself.

What a well-funded rival could not easily reproduce

Not the model, and not the data centre — both are being replicated with capital. What is hard is owning all four layers at once, because each layer owned removes both a supplier's margin and a supplier's queue. Alphabet has DeepMind and Gemini, the TPU, its own data centres, and after the $5.9bn Intersect acquisition in March 2026, a renewable energy developer. Microsoft rents its frontier model and buys merchant silicon; Amazon has custom silicon but no frontier model of its own; Meta has models but no cloud business through which to sell them. On the evidence available Alphabet is the only one of the four that owns every layer — and in an industry where accelerators, grid interconnects and land are the binding constraints, that is the kind of company that wins.

Testing the company's account against outside evidence

  • "AI is not cannibalising Search" — partially supported. Paid clicks are up 13% and StatCounter puts Google at ~89–90% of conventional search, down from ~91% in 2023. But Pew Research Center clickstream data found users clicked a result on 15% of visits without an AI summary and 8% with one, and Apple testified in May 2025 that Safari searches fell for the first time in over 22 years. The query is safe; the click is the exposed variable.
  • "A differentiated full-stack cloud position" — supported by competitors' own numbers.
  • "Demand exceeds supply" — supported. The backlog series and management's repeated compute-constrained statements are disclosures against interest, not marketing.
  • "An unassailable advertising position" — contradicted at the margin. Alphabet earns more than Meta and Amazon combined but grew slower than both in the most recent comparable period.
Google will not be required to divest Chrome; nor will the court include a contingent divestiture of the Android operating system in the final judgment. Plaintiffs overreached.
Judge Amit Mehta, remedies decision in United States v. Google LLC, 2 September 2025. Final judgment entered 5 December 2025; partly effective February 2026; both sides on appeal to the D.C. Circuit.

The practical effect on the moat is smaller than the headline. Google may still pay for placement — the Apple, Samsung and carrier payments continue. What it may not do is sign exclusive defaults, or condition revenue-share on placement for more than a year at a time; it must also license certain search index and user-interaction data, though not advertising data, to qualified competitors. The mechanism the court removed was exclusivity. The mechanism that actually holds the position — users choosing Google when offered a choice — was untouched, and the European choice-screen precedent suggests roughly 97% still do.

4 · Growth engine

Alphabet grew 24% year on year in Q2 2026, its twelfth consecutive quarter of double-digit growth. Revenue rose $23.4bn against the same quarter of 2025. Where it came from matters more than the headline, because two of the four contributors are structural and one is shrinking.

Reported growth versus organic growth

Two acquisitions closed in March 2026 and both sit inside the reported rate. Wiz, bought for $29,467m and consolidated into Google Cloud from 11 March, had crossed $1bn of annual recurring revenue before close according to press reporting — a figure Alphabet does not disclose. At roughly $250–300m a quarter it accounts for perhaps two to three points of Cloud's 82% growth, leaving organic Cloud growth around 79–80% (inferred). Intersect adds power capacity and property, not revenue. At group level the acquisitions contribute well under one point of the 24%. This is overwhelmingly a grown number, not a bought one — but the split is an estimate, because Alphabet does not disaggregate it.

Cloud backlog conversion
Structural

Growth accelerated from 32% in Q2 2025 to 48% in Q4 2025 to 82% in Q2 2026, while backlog went from $108.2bn a year ago to $513.9bn. Roughly half converts within 24 months on management's guidance, so near-term Cloud growth is largely already sold. The constraint is delivery, not demand.

Search query volume
Structural

Search grew 17% in Q2 2026 on clicks up 13% and price up 3% — a volume story with a modest price tail, which is the healthier configuration: more queries are being monetised rather than existing queries repriced. It is also the driver the bear case says should already be failing, and it is not.

United States concentration
Cyclical

The US supplied roughly 63% of the entire year-on-year increase and crossed 50% of group revenue for the first time. US growth went from 11.8% a year earlier to 32.1% while EMEA decelerated from a 21.4% peak to 15.0%. The concentration is partly circular: much of the incremental US demand is AI-sector capital spending, funded by the same cycle Alphabet is selling into.

Consumer subscriptions
Management-driven

Paid subscriptions reached 350m by Q1 2026 from 325m at the end of 2025, led by Google One and YouTube Premium, and YouTube passed $60bn of combined advertising and subscription revenue in FY2025. The most predictable line in the company, and the least discussed.

TPU systems sold to third parties
Management-driven — not yet visible

First deliveries occurred in Q2 2026 and management expects the majority of revenue in 2027, so this is almost entirely absent from current figures. Inventory building from $2.4bn to $10.0bn in six months is the leading indicator. Margins were explicitly refused: "We don't break out margins for any specific products."

5 · Margin, cash & capital allocation

The consolidated 34.0% operating margin in Q2 2026 averages three very different things. Google Services earned $39,544m on $94,540m of revenue; Google Cloud earned $8,814m on $24,768m, up from a 20.7% margin a year earlier; Other Bets lost $1,799m. Against all of it sits $5,789m of unallocated Alphabet-level cost — shared AI research and development plus corporate fines — a line that has grown from $1,299m for the whole of FY2022 to $16,760m in FY2025 and $11,178m in the first half of 2026 alone.

The advertising margin is being helped by mix, not pricing. Traffic acquisition cost fell from 20.6% of Google advertising revenue to 19.8% year on year, not because distribution partners accepted less but because Google Network, which pays away most of its revenue, keeps shrinking. That tailwind ends when Network stops shrinking or becomes too small to matter.

Cloud's expansion is the clearest evidence of operating leverage in the business: in H1 2026 the segment added $10.4bn of operating income on $18.9bn of incremental revenue, an incremental margin of roughly 55%. Management has flagged one offset — leasing third-party capacity as a bridge to its own build "will put some pressure on operating margins for Cloud."

Cash conversion has inverted

Operating cash flow of $185,675m over the last twelve months is the highest in the company's history. Free cash flow of $53,273m is the lowest since before 2021. The quarterly path shows when it broke: $24.5bn in Q3 2025, $24.6bn in Q4, $10.1bn in Q1 2026 and −$5,855m in Q2 2026 — the first negative quarter on record.

The reported figure also understates what has been committed. At 30 June 2026, $29,113m of property and equipment sat in payables rather than cash paid, $122,814m of assets were recorded as not yet in service, and contractual commitments stood at $707.0bn with a further $85.2bn of leases signed but not commenced. Depreciation, the mechanism that converts all of this into the profit and loss account, rose from $15,311m in FY2024 to $21,136m in FY2025 and $13,586m in H1 2026 alone — but capital expenditure is still running at 5.25 times depreciation. The servers carry a six-year life adopted in 2023, unchanged in the Q2 2026 10-Q.

Who has first claim on the cash

Ranked over the last twelve months: capital expenditure $132.4bn, acquisitions $35.3bn, buybacks $17.4bn, dividends $10.3bn, and net debt repayment of nothing. Within H1 2026 the ranking is starker: repurchases were exactly zero against $28,306m a year earlier, with $69.5bn of the April 2025 authorisation unused.

The funding side changed more than the spending side. In June 2026 Alphabet issued equity for cash for the first time in its history — 29m Class A and 29m Class C shares at roughly $353, a $10.0bn private placement to a Berkshire Hathaway affiliate, and 19m shares of 6.25% mandatory convertible preferred, for net proceeds of $49.6bn — and authorised a $40.0bn at-the-market programme, undrawn at 30 June. Long-term debt face value went from $49,085m to $101,085m across six currencies, and to roughly $126.1bn after a $25.0bn issue on 10 August 2026 that post-dates the reported figures.

What the ranking reveals is a deliberate reordering of claims. Internal reinvestment comes first and shareholders come last, to the point where the company is issuing stock at the same time as it has stopped buying it back. Diluted shares rose from 12,198m to 12,309m, and the stock plan reserve grew by 200m Class C shares in June. This is behaviour, not a verdict: management is buying an option on being the infrastructure provider of the next decade, and is willing to dilute existing holders to pay for it.

The other half of reported earnings

Diluted EPS of $9.11 is not an operating number. Alphabet booked a $99,031m gain on equity securities in the quarter and $135,946m in H1, and states directly that $6.26 of the $9.11 came from that gain along with $21.9bn of tax. Over the last twelve months these marks are close to half of pre-tax income. Roughly $94.1bn of the position is SpaceX stock, restricted from sale in tranches through Q3 2027; the rest is a non-marketable holding the filings identify only as "a private company," widely reported to be Anthropic though named in no filing (inferred). The same line was a $5,519m loss in FY2022.

6 · Cyclicality, constraints & what to monitor

Alphabet has an advertising cycle it has already survived and a capital cycle it has never experienced. The advertising trough is documented: in Q4 2022 revenue growth fell to 1.0% and the operating margin to 23.9%, and revenue never actually declined. That is the downside case for the advertising business, drawn from its own record rather than a forecast, and it is survivable.

Against that record the business today sits near the top on every measure — 24.2% growth, a 34.0% margin. But the margin peak has already passed: it reached 36.1% in Q1 2026 and fell 2.1 points sequentially, with operating income growing 2.7% quarter on quarter while revenue grew 9.0%. A record margin at a peak and a record margin at a trough are opposite facts, and this is the former, one quarter after the turn.

The downside mechanism this business would actually feel is not an advertising recession. It is that depreciation on a six-year server life arrives on a fixed schedule while backlog conversion depends on delivery and utilisation. At 5.25 times depreciation the charge is effectively contracted for; the revenue that justifies it is not. If utilisation of the 2026–2028 vintages disappoints, the margin compresses on a timetable management can only alter by revisiting the useful-life assumption — an accounting judgement, not an operating fix. Server useful lives were not discussed on any of the last five earnings calls, and no analyst raised them.

Durable — likely intact in ten years

  • The click-relevance feedback loop across five trillion-plus annual queries
  • Owned distribution via Android and Chrome, unaffected by the search remedies
  • $513.9bn of contracted Cloud backlog, roughly 6.6× LTM segment revenue
  • Vertical integration from model to accelerator to data centre to power
  • A ~42% operating margin in Google Services, sustained across a full ad cycle

Borrowed — currently helping, will not persist

  • Equity-securities marks at roughly half of LTM pre-tax income; the same line was a $5.5bn loss in FY2022
  • Depreciation running at one-fifth of capital expenditure; the charge is deferred, not avoided
  • A falling TAC rate driven by Google Network's decay rather than renegotiation
  • A US-led growth surge — 63% of Q2's increase — financed by the same AI capital cycle Alphabet sells into
  • Interest expense not yet reflecting $126bn of debt; the August issue alone adds roughly $1.4bn a year

7 · Risks, unknowns & questions for deeper work

Cyclicality and the depreciation schedule are above. What follows is what those do not capture, ordered by how much the risks compound one another.

  • The ad-tech remedy is undecided, and it is the one that could remove an asset. Judge Brinkema found in April 2025 that Google's publisher tools unlawfully excluded rivals. Remedies were argued in September 2025, closing arguments were heard in November, and no judgment had issued as of the Q2 2026 10-Q. The DOJ sought structural remedies and Alphabet states these "could have a material adverse effect." The honest read is two-sided: Google Network is $29.8bn of FY2025 revenue, so divestiture removes about 7% of revenue — but it is the lowest-margin 7%, and removing it would raise the group margin.
  • The click, not the query, is the billable event. Every query Gemini answers completely is a query that produced no auction. Pew's 15%-to-8% click-rate finding describes the mechanism precisely. Clicks are still growing 13%, so this has not happened — but the metric to watch is not query volume, which Alphabet emphasises, nor engine share, which is stable. It is paid clicks, reported only as a percentage change.
  • The equity marks can reverse, and Alphabet is now issuing equity against them. Non-marketable equity carried at $124.3bn was written up $87.9bn in a single quarter on third-party funding rounds, which are procyclical by construction. A reversal would not touch operating income but would hit reported earnings and shareholders' equity — and $20.0bn of contingent funding to the same counterparty runs through 2030.
  • Dilution and depreciation compound. The ATM removes the share shrinkage that net settlement of employee equity used to provide; the stock plan reserve grew 200m shares; diluted shares are already rising. Set that against a depreciation charge scheduled to grow faster than revenue and earnings per share can fall while revenue grows. Neither element alone is alarming; together they are the mechanism.
  • Backlog concentration is undisclosed. There is $513.9bn of Cloud backlog and $707.0bn of supply commitments, and no customer concentration disclosure for the Cloud segment at all. If a material share of the backlog sits with a few AI laboratories funded by the same capital cycle funding Alphabet's build, the asset and the obligation are correlated. Nothing in the filings establishes this; nothing rules it out.

What the sources could not answer

These are findings, not gaps in the research. Each would need resolving before an investor could form a thesis.

  • Absolute paid clicks and revenue per click — only percentage changes are disclosed.
  • Google Cloud customer concentration — no segment-level disclosure exists.
  • The maintenance versus growth split of capital expenditure — not disclosed, so sustainable post-build free cash flow cannot be estimated.
  • TPU system revenue and its gross margin — explicitly refused on the Q2 2026 call.
  • Wiz's revenue contribution — not disaggregated, so organic Cloud growth is an estimate.
  • The identity of the private company behind $124.3bn of non-marketable holdings — named in no filing.
  • Whether a six-year server life still reflects AI-era hardware economics — no change disclosed, question never raised on a call.
  • Segment revenue by geography — reported only at consolidated level, so the US surge cannot be attributed between advertising and cloud.
  • The share of AI-assistant usage displacing search — published estimates range from under 1% of referral traffic to roughly 17% of digital queries; the spread is too wide for any figure to be usable.

8 · Investor takeaways

  • What this business really is. No longer an advertising company that happens to own a cloud business, but an advertising business funding the construction of an AI infrastructure business — and the construction now determines the reported numbers.
  • The core economic engine. An auction that retains roughly 80c of every advertising dollar after distribution costs and converts 42c of every revenue dollar to operating profit, attached to a cloud business now converting 36c and growing at 82%.
  • The main growth lever. Conversion of $513.9bn of contracted Cloud backlog, roughly half of it scheduled within 24 months. Near-term cloud growth is largely already sold; the constraint is delivery.
  • What could break the story. Depreciation on a six-year life arriving faster than the utilisation that justifies it, while the buyback stays off and the share count rises — making falling earnings per share on rising revenue an arithmetic possibility rather than a bearish opinion.
  • What to monitor. Quarterly Cloud backlog additions, which fell from roughly $220bn to $54bn between Q1 and Q2 2026; paid-click growth, currently 13%; and depreciation as a percentage of revenue.
