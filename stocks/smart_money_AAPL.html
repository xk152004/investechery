Wedgewood Partners David Rolfe
Confirmed current owner High
Fund letter — quarterly (Q2 2026) · 2026-07-31 · Position: 7.81% of portfolio (130,682 shares, $37.81M); 7.20% average weight in Q2 2026 as of 2026-06-30
Thesis summary
Apple is a two-decade core holding held as a quality compounder rather than a trade. "we've owned Alphabet and Apple for two decades" — Wedgewood Partners Q2 2026 Client Letter, July 2026. [Paraphrased from Wedgewood Q2 2026 and Q4 2025 client letters] Wedgewood's case rests on Apple's consistency of hardware/software execution and its ability to keep converting that into upgrade and trade-up demand; the firm manages the position's weight against valuation rather than trading the thesis.
Business quality
"Apple's multi-decade consistency in executing on hardware and software upgrades, along with increasingly proprietary silicon content, provides consumers with a dependable, high-quality user experience" — Wedgewood Partners Q4 2025 Client Letter, January 2026. [Paraphrased from the same letter] Wedgewood links that experience directly to the company's ability to drive adoption and trade-up across the installed base.
Growth drivers
Q2 2026 letter: "Revenues grew 17%, driven by 22% growth in iPhone and 16% growth in services." On the product cycle: "The iPhone 17 family has catalyzed a solid upgrade cycle ahead of what we expect to be another strong launch later this year, featuring a new foldable form factor" — Wedgewood Partners Q2 2026 Client Letter, July 2026. Q4 2025 letter cites "+15% revenue growth in its services business" on over $100B of trailing-twelve-month services revenue, and "double-digit growth in iPhone revenues, as it experienced strong demand after launching several new models," with the company having "guided to strong double-digit revenue growth in its holiday quarter" — Wedgewood Partners Q4 2025 Client Letter, January 2026.
Fundamental drivers
Three specific levers are named across the two letters. (1) iPhone unit/upgrade cycle economics — 22% iPhone revenue growth in the June 2026 quarter, with a foldable launch identified as the next catalyst. (2) Services revenue growth as a mix-and-margin lever — 16% growth in Q2 2026, 15% growth on a >$100B base in Q4 2025. (3) Input-cost management and vertical integration as a gross-margin defence: "As one of the largest single purchasers of DRAM, the Company has strong negotiating leverage, but it can also implement hardware and software innovations to reduce its dependence on memory" — Wedgewood Partners Q2 2026 Client Letter, July 2026. Contribution to return in Q2 2026 was 0.95 percentage points on a 7.20% average weight; in Q4 2025 it was +0.50% on a 7.38% average weight, and +0.42% for full-year 2025 on a 7.01% average weight.
Moat / competitive advantage
"increasingly proprietary silicon content" combined with "multi-decade consistency in executing on hardware and software upgrades" — Wedgewood Partners Q4 2025 Client Letter, January 2026. The Q2 2026 letter adds scale-based supplier leverage: Apple as "one of the largest single purchasers of DRAM" with "strong negotiating leverage," plus the engineering option to "reduce its dependence on memory" through hardware and software design.
Management
unknown — the letters reviewed do not comment directly on Apple's management team, incentives or capital allocation.
Valuation angle
Wedgewood manages the weight, not the thesis. [Paraphrased from Wedgewood Partners Q4 2025 Client Letter, January 2026] The firm trimmed Apple during Q4 2025, grouping it among positions it viewed as due for a valuation pause or retreat after two consecutive strong years — a valuation-driven trim, not a change in the fundamental view. The Q2 2026 13F shows a further 3.29% reduction in share count. In the Q2 2026 letter Apple is grouped with other 'Magnificent 7' holdings the firm believes remain attractive despite market headwinds affecting hyperscalers.
Risks mentioned
[Paraphrased from Wedgewood Partners Q2 2026 Client Letter, July 2026] Memory (DRAM) cost inflation is the risk the letter engages with most directly, framed as a bill-of-materials pressure Apple can partially offset through purchasing leverage and design. Valuation is treated as the second risk, evidenced by the Q4 2025 trim rationale rather than by an explicit risk statement.
Time horizon
Long. "we've owned Alphabet and Apple for two decades" — Q2 2026 letter; 13F data shows continuous disclosure since at least Q2 2013.
Evidence notes
Three T1 primary sources on the manager's own domain (Q2 2026, Q1 2026, Q4 2025 client letters). The Q1 2026 letter was checked and contains no Apple discussion — noted so the absence is not mistaken for an unread source. Position data cross-checked against the Q2 2026 13F.

4. Consensus & Divergence

Where investors agree

The verified holder base is united by duration, not by argument. Both investors with published commentary — Wedgewood Partners and Berkshire Hathaway — hold Apple as a very long-duration core position (Wedgewood "for two decades" and disclosed since Q2 2013; Berkshire since Q1 2016) and both frame it as a quality business rather than a cyclical or event-driven trade. Beyond that the agreement is thin, because the agreement is thin in the source material: of eleven verified holders, only two have published anything substantive about Apple in the freshness window. Note also that duration is the pattern across the whole table even where no thesis exists — Greenbrier Partners (21.6% of portfolio) and Muhlenkamp have held since at least Q2 2013, and 7G Capital since Q4 2020. What the smart-money map for Apple actually shows is a base of long-duration owners who have largely stopped explaining themselves.

Where investors split

Driver-specific quality thesis, actively sized against valuation
Wedgewood Partners
Wedgewood names its levers — iPhone cycle economics, services growth, DRAM purchasing leverage, proprietary silicon — and manages the weight against price. It trimmed in Q4 2025 on the view that the position was due for a valuation pause after two strong years, and cut the share count a further 3.29% in Q2 2026, while continuing to describe the business favourably. Thesis intact, position size negotiable.
Portfolio-level quality thesis, deliberate inactivity
Berkshire Hathaway Inc.
Abel's first annual letter places Apple in a category — understandable American businesses expected to compound over decades — without analysing the company. The stated policy is "limited activity in these holdings," with material change reserved for "fundamental changes in its long-term economic prospects," i.e. explicitly not for valuation. The Q2 2026 13F is consistent: the share count is unchanged versus Q1 2026, ending the 2024-2025 trimming programme.
Time-shifted divergence in the commentary itself
Berkshire Hathaway Inc., Wedgewood Partners
The clearest split is not bull versus bear but specific versus generic, and it has widened over time. Berkshire's public case on Apple was once detailed and Buffett-voiced; in the February 2026 letter, under a new CEO, it is a single clause in a list of four names. Wedgewood's has stayed granular and, if anything, become more operational — the Q2 2026 letter argues about memory pricing. An investor relying on Berkshire's disclosure for an Apple view is now relying on considerably less than they were two years ago.
Position weight versus published conviction — the two do not line up
H&H International Investment, Greenbrier Partners Capital Management, 7G Capital Management, Wedgewood Partners
The three largest Apple weights in the verified set — Duan Yongping's H&H at 41.05%, Greenbrier at 21.60%, 7G Capital at 13.24% — produce no citable commentary at all, while the most substantive thesis comes from the fifth-largest weight. Duan's commentary exists but lives on Xueqiu, below this skill's evidence bar. Anyone reading the ownership table as a conviction ranking would be reading it backwards.

6. Research Questions

  1. Wedgewood is the only verified holder making a gross-margin argument, and it is a DRAM-cost argument. Does Apple's own disclosure support the claim that purchasing leverage plus design substitution offsets memory inflation, and over what lag?
  2. Services grew 16% in the June 2026 quarter on a base above $100B. How much of that is App Store take-rate and Google TAC — the two lines most exposed to regulatory and contractual change — versus subscription lines Apple controls outright?
  3. Berkshire's share count was unchanged in Q2 2026 after two years of trimming. Is that a decision or an absence of one? Nothing in Abel's letter distinguishes the two, and it matters for how much signal to read into the pause.
  4. Duan Yongping holds 41% of his book in Apple and trimmed 6.4% in Q2 2026. His reasoning is public but only on Xueqiu — worth reading in the original rather than through intermediaries, since no source at this skill's evidence bar carries it.
  5. The foldable iPhone Wedgewood expects later this year is the only near-term catalyst any verified holder names. What does the bill of materials and expected ASP do to blended hardware gross margin if it ships at volume?
  6. Nine of eleven verified holders publish nothing about Apple despite meaningful weights. For the ones that do write regularly and chose not to mention it — Matrix, Muhlenkamp, Mairs & Power — check prior-year letters to see whether Apple was ever discussed, or whether it has always been a position they hold without arguing for.
