SK hynix is a bit factory. It converts silicon wafers into units of memory in fabs that run flat out, and sells those units at a price set by global supply and demand — with no backlog, no contracted price, and nothing between the market and its income statement.
The economic engine
One bit of memory

Almost the entire cost of producing that bit is fixed within the quarter — depreciation of ₩13,890bn in 2025 on plant already built, and a workforce averaging 12.1 years of service. Raw materials, wafers included, were ₩10,970bn of purchases in the first half of 2026 against ₩131,895bn of revenue. So every Won of price increase falls almost intact to operating profit, and every Won of decrease falls out of it the same way. In 2025 revenue rose 46.8% while cost of sales rose 11.9%. In 2023, the same fabs and the same people produced a gross margin of minus 1.6%.

1. What the company does

Every computer has a bottleneck between the processor that does the arithmetic and the place the data sits while it waits. SK hynix sells the place the data sits. DRAM holds what the processor needs in the next few nanoseconds and forgets it when the power goes off; NAND flash holds what the machine needs to keep. In an AI data centre both have become scarce at once, because a graphics processor capable of a trillion operations a second is worthless if memory cannot feed it fast enough — the constraint the industry calls the memory wall.

Trace a single bit. It begins as part of a 300mm silicon wafer bought from suppliers in Japan, Korea, Germany and the United States; wafers are roughly 10% of cost of sales. It is patterned into a die at Icheon or Wuxi (DRAM) or Cheongju or Dalian (NAND), then sent to a back-end plant at Icheon, Cheongju or Chongqing to be tested, stacked and packaged into a module, an HBM stack or a solid-state drive. It is sold, almost always in US dollars, to a hyperscaler, a server maker or a handset manufacturer, at a volume and price agreed monthly or quarterly.

The company states plainly that it has no order backlog. That single fact explains the shape of everything downstream: with no backlog and no long-dated contracted price, there is no cushion between the spot market and the income statement. When the price of a bit rises, revenue rises the same quarter. When it falls, it falls the same quarter.

The mix has narrowed towards DRAM — 63.4% of revenue in 2023, 67.6% in 2024, 77.1% in 2025 and 77.3% in the first quarter of 2026, while NAND fell from 29.5% to 22.0% and everything else collapsed from 7.2% to 0.7%. Part of that is arithmetic, because DRAM prices rose faster. Part is deliberate: in March 2025 the company decided to move its CMOS image sensor division into AI memory, and other-product revenue fell 29.0% to ₩1,552bn in 2025 as image sensor sales declined. Because semiconductor revenue exceeds 90% of the total, the company reports a single segment — so DRAM profitability cannot be separated from NAND, and HBM does not appear in the financial statements at all.

2. Industry, competitive position and moat

Memory is a commodity with a specification. A bit of DDR5 from one supplier is functionally interchangeable with a bit from another once it has passed the customer's qualification. That interchangeability is why the industry has almost no pricing power at the product level and enormous pricing power at the industry level: when total supply is short every supplier's price rises together, and when it is long every supplier's price falls together. The 2025 memory market was US$220.1bn of a US$809.0bn semiconductor market, DRAM US$146.9bn and NAND US$68.1bn (Gartner, June 2026, via company filings).

The profit pool sits with the integrated device manufacturers who own both the process technology and the fabs. Three of them hold roughly 90% of DRAM revenue, with CXMT of China growing 716% year on year off a small base in the second quarter of 2026. NAND is more fragmented — Samsung 28%, SK hynix 19%, Micron 15%, Kioxia 14%, YMTC 14%, SanDisk 11% (Counterpoint Research, Q2 2026). The market is global rather than regional, because bits ship anywhere at negligible freight cost, so share figures mean what they appear to mean.

The barrier that binds is not capital, which several state-backed entrants have. It is the pairing of process yield with customer qualification: a new HBM supplier must persuade an accelerator designer to requalify an entire memory subsystem. The company describes deep customisation and integration as creating substantial switching costs. Outside evidence partially supports that and partially contradicts it.

Samsung took four quarters to move from 15% to 33% of HBM revenue while SK hynix fell from 64% to 50%. Qualification is genuinely hard — it took four quarters, not one — but it gates a programme, not a customer relationship, and it has now been passed. Counterpoint also notes that HBM3E prices declined year on year and that HBM4 launch delays pressured pricing.

The same test applied to conventional DRAM is less flattering. The company reports IDC data showing its DRAM share at 29.9% in 2023, 33.4% in 2024, 34.8% in 2025 and 29.1% in the first quarter of 2026. Counterpoint's independent series puts it at 26% in the second quarter of 2026 against Samsung at 39% and Micron at 25%, down from 39% a year earlier. The providers use different bases and are not comparable in level, but they agree on direction: SK hynix has lost relative share through the upcycle while setting revenue records. The mechanism is in the company's own disclosure — its fabs ran at 100% utilisation throughout the first half, so it had no incremental bits to sell into a market growing 57% quarter on quarter.

Three companies with different technology positions, different product mixes and different capital bases are all earning between 70% and 85% operating margins. That is not evidence of anyone's moat. It is the price of a bit, arriving at three income statements at once.
SK hynix 76.3% (Q2 2026) · Samsung Device Solutions 70.0%, ₩89.2tn on ₩127.5tn (Q2 2026) · Micron 80.4% GAAP on US$41,456m (quarter to 28 May 2026), guiding to ~86% gross margin

What wins in this industry over a full cycle is therefore not the highest margin at the peak. It is the lowest cost per bit entering the downturn, a balance sheet that can fund capital spending through a loss-making year, and a mix weighted towards whatever stays scarce longest. SK hynix is credibly two of those three: net cash, and weighted towards HBM and server DRAM, which TrendForce expects to stay short into 2027. Whether it has the lowest cost per bit is disclosed by no one and cannot be established from these sources.

3. Growth engine

Revenue grew 256.8% year on year in the second quarter of 2026, to ₩79,318,746m. Almost none of that is volume, and none of it is acquired. The Intel NAND business has been consolidated since December 2021 — the final US$2.2bn instalment paid in March 2025 was a payment, not a new consolidation — and SK keyfoundry was bought for ₩576bn in August 2022. Neither changed the reporting perimeter in 2025 or 2026. Reported growth and organic growth are the same number.

Ranked drivers

Cyclical
DRAM average selling price

The dominant driver by a wide margin. Prices are set by industry supply and demand, not by the company; server DRAM contract prices were forecast to rise a further 13–18% quarter on quarter in 3Q26 (TrendForce, 9 July 2026). This lever reverses as readily as it rises.

Structural
Mix shift into HBM and server DRAM

HBM stacks many DRAM dies into one package and sells at a large premium per bit. The company held 50–58% of HBM revenue through the period. Mix is what makes SK hynix's realised ASP move differently from the commodity index.

Cyclical, with a structural core
NAND price and the enterprise SSD mix

NAND ASP rose in the mid-50% range in 2Q26 with bit volume up in the mid-10% range, helped by a weak first-quarter base and expanded eSSD sales. The structural part — AI inference offloading key-value cache from memory to SSD — is real. The price part is not.

Management-driven
Capacity additions

The M15X extension fab in Cheongju opened its cleanroom in October 2025 and began wafer input in 1Q26. This is the only lever management directly controls, and it takes years: Yongin phase 1 opens its cleanroom in 1Q27, the Indiana packaging plant in 2H28.

Temporary
A weaker Won

The company reports in Won and sells in dollars. The average rate moved from ₩1,363.4 per US$1.00 in 2024 to ₩1,421.4 in 2025, and the company names this as a contributor to the 2025 increase in DRAM and NAND revenue in Won terms.

4. Margin, cash and capital allocation

The 2025 accounts show the operating leverage precisely: revenue rose 46.8% to ₩97,147bn while cost of sales rose 11.9% to ₩38,456bn, taking gross margin from 48.1% to 60.4%. In the first quarter of 2026 revenue rose 198.1% year on year while cost of sales rose 44.6%, and gross margin reached 79.3%. One cost behaves unusually and is worth naming: salaries inside cost of sales rise sharply in good years because of a profit-sharing incentive programme, which the company cites as a main reason for the cost increase in both 2025 and 1Q26. It is a genuine variable cost that self-corrects in a downturn, so the record margin is already net of a large bonus accrual.

Why profit for the period is not the earnings power

Taken at face value, that fourth row is misleading. First-half profit of ₩134,269bn sits against operating profit of ₩98,153bn. The gap, and more, is net finance income of ₩76,251bn — of which ₩63,179bn is a gain on the valuation of financial instruments and ₩13,980bn is dividend income. The Level 3 fair value note isolates it: long-term investment assets opened the year at ₩14,547bn, took a valuation gain of ₩62,944bn, disposed of ₩10,656bn and had ₩66,077bn transferred out to Level 2 as the valuation methodology changed. The balance sheet line stood at ₩85,310bn at 30 June 2026 against ₩14,547bn six months earlier.

These are the Kioxia-linked holdings (inferred, from the amounts and the disclosed portfolio). SK hynix invested ₩2,637bn for a limited partnership interest in a Bain-led vehicle in 2018 and ₩1,279bn in a convertible bond of a second vehicle convertible into roughly 15.0% of it; carrying values at 31 March 2026 were ₩6,616bn and ₩13,609bn, and the first vehicle sold its remaining Kioxia equity in June 2026. Kioxia shares rose roughly 2,220% in the year to May 2026, at which point Korean press valued the SK hynix interest above ₩60tn against a carrying value near ₩14tn at the end of 2025. The gain is real, very large, non-cash, non-repeating and unrelated to selling memory. Operating profit of ₩98,153bn is the number that describes the memory business; a reversal would run back through profit or loss.

A smaller distortion runs the other way. Finance expenses include a ₩3,979bn loss on derivatives from exchangeable bonds issued in April 2023, arising because the company's own share price rose and the exchange rights were exercised. The company states there is no cash outflow and that the loss is offset by treasury-share disposal gains in equity, so the net effect on total equity is minimal.

Cash conversion is strong but not one-for-one, because price rises inflate receivables before they are collected: trade receivables went from ₩18,199bn to ₩47,821bn and inventories from ₩14,289bn to ₩17,986bn over the half. The balance sheet has been transformed — total equity ₩262,693bn against ₩120,667bn six months earlier, borrowings down to ₩18,587bn, cash and equivalents ₩87,958bn, and liabilities-to-equity at 32.8% against 87.5% at the end of 2023.

Where the cash has gone, ranked

  • Capital expenditure — ₩70,119bn from 2023 to June 2026, with 2026 spending planned to rise considerably above 2025. Committed projects at 31 March 2026: Yongin fab 1 phase 1 ₩9,412bn and phases 2–6 ₩21,608bn (construction only); P&T7 advanced packaging in Cheongju ₩19,000bn including equipment; Indiana packaging ₩5,900bn, supported by up to US$458m of CHIPS Act subsidies and US$570m of loans.
  • Share cancellation — an estimated ₩40,004bn, announced 19 August 2026. The board resolved to buy treasury shares in the open market between 20 August and 19 November 2026 and cancel them, sized at 24,070,000 shares against 730,492,365 issued, using the prior day's close of ₩1,662,000. This post-dates every reported figure above and is roughly a third of first-half operating profit.
  • Acquisitions — US$8.8bn for the Intel NAND business (US$6.6bn December 2021, US$2.2bn March 2025) and ₩576bn for SK keyfoundry in August 2022.
  • Dividends — a fixed ₩1,500 per share per year under the policy set in November 2024 for 2025–2027, in four ₩375 instalments; the Q2 2026 dividend was ₩273bn. On 7 August 2026 the company said it is reviewing additional shareholder returns and expects to announce them within the third quarter.
  • Debt reduction — borrowings from ₩29,469bn at end-2023 to ₩18,587bn, alongside US$26.2bn of net proceeds from the July 2026 Nasdaq listing of 17,790,000 new shares (2.50% of the share count) at US$149.00 per American Depositary Share.

The ranking says something specific. A fixed dividend set in November 2024 has not been raised despite a fivefold increase in profitability, while the variable return came through a one-off cancellation announced only once the cash had arrived, and capital expenditure was allowed to triple. Capacity first, balance sheet second, variable returns third, fixed commitments last — the allocation of a company that expects to need cash in a downturn.

5. Cyclicality, constraints and what to monitor

This business sits at or very near the top of its cycle on all three axes that matter. On price, DRAM ASP has risen quarter on quarter for six consecutive quarters. On volume, the fabs ran at 100% utilisation for the whole first half — available working hours of 136,255,098 exactly equalled actual working hours, which is the definition of a ceiling. On margin, 76.3% is an all-time record set against a trough three years earlier at minus 23.6%. A record margin at a peak and a record margin at a trough are opposite facts, and this is the first kind.

The binding constraint follows from the utilisation figure: there is no volume lever left. Any further revenue growth in 2026 must come from price or mix, because the next meaningful capacity does not arrive until the Yongin phase 1 cleanroom in 1Q27 — and a cleanroom is not production; equipment installation and qualification follow. TrendForce expects new industry capacity to contribute materially only from 2H27 and substantially from 2028, partly because HBM consumes far more wafer input per bit than conventional DRAM, so wafer starts now translate into fewer sellable bits than they used to.

That supply arithmetic makes the downside asymmetric between the two products. TrendForce puts the DRAM sufficiency ratio at minus 1% to minus 2% for 2026 with the gap widening in 2027, so the DRAM squeeze persists. NAND is the opposite: higher-layer migration and new fabs ramping are expected to turn NAND sufficiency positive in 2027, shifting to a balanced market while consumer demand stays subdued. NAND was 22.0% of Q1 2026 revenue and its ASP rose in the mid-50% range in Q2. If the NAND band flips negative before DRAM does, roughly a fifth of revenue starts deflating while the rest is still inflating — and against a fixed cost base, a fifth of revenue is a far larger fraction of profit.

A second mechanism caps the upside on price even while supply stays short. Several large US cloud providers hold multi-year agreements that restrict suppliers from raising prices to them, pushing increases onto customers without such agreements (TrendForce, 9 July 2026); Counterpoint attributes part of Samsung's second-quarter strength to rivals being locked into earlier agreements at lower negotiated levels. With the United States at 64.7% of revenue, SK hynix is more exposed to that dynamic than its market share alone suggests.

Durable — survives a full cycle

  • HBM stacking know-how (TSV, MR-MUF) and qualified-supplier status with AI accelerator makers
  • An advanced-node fab base at Icheon, Cheongju, Wuxi and Dalian that costs tens of trillions of Won to reproduce
  • Net cash: ₩87,958bn of cash and equivalents against ₩18,587bn of borrowings
  • 321-layer QLC NAND and the Solidigm enterprise SSD line-up acquired from Intel
  • 22,236 intellectual property rights and a Rambus cross-licence running to June 2034

Borrowed — currently helping

  • The price level itself: DRAM ASP up in five of the last six quarters, NAND in four
  • The ₩63tn Kioxia-linked valuation gain, which is non-cash and does not repeat
  • 100% utilisation, which flatters unit cost and leaves no capacity to sell into further price rises
  • A weaker Won, which lifts reported revenue without lifting a single bit
  • R&D at 4.6% of H1 2026 sales against 7.5% in 2024 — a denominator effect, not restraint

6. Risks, unknowns and questions for deeper work

These are the risks that cyclicality alone does not capture; cycle position and the utilisation ceiling are covered above.

  • China manufacturing runs on a licence renewed annually at another government's discretion. The validated end-user authorisations that let foreign-owned fabs in China receive US-controlled equipment without per-shipment licences expired on 31 December 2025 and were replaced by annual export licences. Samsung and SK hynix received 2026 licences, but the framework supports operation and maintenance rather than expansion, and each renewal is a fresh decision. Wuxi (DRAM) and Dalian (NAND) sit inside it. A refusal or narrowing would not shut them immediately; it would freeze their technology node while competitors migrate, which converts a producing asset into a depreciating one.
  • A regulatory condition caps NAND pricing in a quarter of the revenue base. China's market regulator conditioned approval of the Intel NAND acquisition on maintaining a reasonable pricing policy and production level in the Chinese enterprise SSD market for five years from December 2021. The company states in its own prospectus that it expects this to limit its ability to significantly raise NAND prices in China in 2026. China was 24.3% of Q1 2026 revenue. The condition expires in December 2026 and any waiver beyond that is at the regulator's discretion.
  • Customer concentration turns a commercial negotiation into a political one. The largest customer was 23.9% of FY2025 revenue; the two largest were 14.8% and 12.4% of Q1 2026 revenue. The company's own risk disclosure goes further than concentration: it states that having to allocate finite supply among customers may damage those relationships and attract heightened political, legal and regulatory scrutiny. A supplier who must choose which hyperscaler gets the bits is exposed in a way a supplier with spare capacity is not.
  • Depreciation on ₩70tn of capital spending arrives after the price peak, not with it. Depreciation begins when a plant completes, and the largest tranche — Yongin, P&T7, Indiana — is still pre-production. KPMG identified the commencement timing of depreciation for machinery and equipment as a critical audit matter for the 2025 statements, a direct signal that the timing involves judgement and is material. If prices normalise as this depreciation lands, margin falls faster than price. This compounds with the utilisation constraint: the capacity creating the depreciation is the same capacity arriving too late to sell into the current price.
  • Three announced but undetermined transactions could each move the perimeter. The company has confirmed to the Korea Exchange that it is reviewing, without having decided, a pre-IPO capital raising of approximately ₩5tn at its Solidigm subsidiary, a sale of an ownership stake in its Chongqing packaging plant reported at approximately ₩4tn, and a semiconductor facility in Japan. In each case it stated that no matters have been determined. All three post-date the reported financial statements.

What these sources could not answer

  • Absolute bit shipments and dollars per gigabyte. Only quarter-on-quarter percentage bands are disclosed, so an outsider cannot build a unit-economics model, separate mix from price within the ASP move, or compare cost per bit with Samsung and Micron.
  • Profitability by product. With one reported segment, DRAM margin cannot be separated from NAND, and HBM revenue and margin are not disclosed at all. The most important product in the business is invisible in the financial statements.
  • The maintenance-versus-growth split of capital expenditure, and any Won figure for 2026 capital spending — the guidance is only that it will rise considerably above 2025.
  • Q2 2026 gross margin and cost of sales. Korean rules permit the management discussion to be omitted from semi-annual reports and the company omitted it, so the most recent quarter has revenue and operating profit but no cost detail.
  • Earnings call transcripts and investor day materials, which are not filed on EDGAR. Management's framing of long-term targets, and the tone of analyst pushback, are absent from this evidence base entirely.
  • The identity of the two largest customers, and the terms of any long-term agreements governing price with them.

Before forming a view, four things would need resolving: what SK hynix's cost per bit is relative to Samsung's and Micron's, since that determines who survives the next trough intact; what proportion of revenue is covered by price-capped long-term agreements; how much of the HBM share loss is programme timing rather than a durable second-source decision by the accelerator makers; and what the depreciation schedule looks like as Yongin, P&T7 and Indiana come into service.

7. Investor takeaways

  • What this business really is: a two-product bit factory — DRAM 77%, NAND 22% — with no backlog and no contracted price, whose earnings are made by an industry price it does not set.
  • The core economic engine: fixed-cost fabs at 100% utilisation. In 2025 cost of sales rose 11.9% while revenue rose 46.8%; that gap is the whole business, and it runs in both directions.
  • The main growth lever: DRAM average selling price, and the HBM and server mix that lifts it above the commodity index. Volume has been flat to slightly up for four quarters.
  • What could break the story: HBM ceasing to be scarce — Samsung moved from 15% to 33% of HBM revenue in four quarters — or NAND supply normalising in 2027, either landing alongside depreciation on ₩70tn of new capacity.
  • What to monitor: the quarterly ASP and bit-volume bands in the company's own 6-K filings, HBM share against Samsung, and the annual US export licence covering Wuxi and Dalian.
