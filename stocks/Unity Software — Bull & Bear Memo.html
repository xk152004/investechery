Unity Software — Bull & Bear Memo

Thinking anchor, not a valuation. Written from company sources as of Q2 FY2026.

1. Business in one line

Unity sells the engine most mobile games are built on (Create: subscriptions priced per seat, with minimum enterprise commitments; about 29% of Q2'26 revenue), and it runs an AI ad network that helps those same games buy new players (Grow: Unity keeps a cut of each ad auction; about 71%).
Turnaround, turning into a fast grower

Consolidated revenue grew 2% in FY2025 [10-K], but the strategic business grew 38% in Q2'26 once ironSource and Supersonic were exited [Q2'26 call]. For a turnaround, the question is whether the new engine (Vector) keeps compounding or was a one-time catch-up. That is where the bear case sits.

2. Bull case — Peter Lynch pitch

The simple reason this stock could work is that Unity has turned its engine's reach into a performance-ad business that is growing 60%+ on 83% adjusted gross margins, and the old businesses that were hiding it are now gone.

A1Vector keeps winning share of mobile-game ad spend
What must happen
Vector needs to keep delivering better return on ad spend (ROAS) than rival networks, so advertisers move budget to it every quarter. Sequential growth needs to stay in the teens for another 4–6 quarters.
Why Unity specifically
Performance-ad spend follows measured returns. Unity ships model updates quickly (20+ Vector updates in Q2) and has added new products such as day-28 ROAS campaigns, which grew almost 3x from Q1 and are used by more than 25% of advertisers. Only $3M of Q2 Vector growth came from former ironSource customers.
Evidence
Vector grew 23% quarter over quarter in Q2'26 against a 12–13% plan, after four straight quarters of about 15%. Its annual run-rate is 'substantially over $1B'. Strategic Grow revenue was $329M, up 63% [Q2'26 call]. Q3 guide: Vector +19–21% quarter over quarter, Grow +68–70% year over year.
What to monitor
Vector sequential growth, and strategic Grow growth year over year.
A2Runtime data is a signal rivals can't buy
What must happen
Player-behaviour data from the Unity runtime needs to make Vector's predictions measurably better, and developers need to keep opting in.
Why Unity specifically
About 3 billion people a month play a Made-with-Unity game [Q2'26 call]. No other ad network sees in-game behaviour at the engine level across that many titles. This is what could turn a model race into a moat.
Evidence
Runtime signals went into production models only at the end of Q2'26, so this is still early. Opt-in to the developer data framework is above 90% [Q1'26 call].
What to monitor
Opt-in rate, any disclosure on runtime's contribution to returns, and Apple/Google policy changes.
A3The engine stays the default through the AI transition and feeds the ad flywheel
What must happen
Unity needs to hold its roughly 70% share of mobile game creation [Q1'26 call] as coding agents change how games get built. Pricing needs to move from seats to agent usage and consumption without revenue leaking out.
Why Unity specifically
Unity 7 (beta Q4'26, full release Q1'27) opens the engine to coding agents through a free MCP server and open APIs. It also switches on Vector, commerce and live-ops automatically, so new games start inside the Grow funnel. Unity AI pricing now counts agent connections as well as seats.
Evidence
Strategic Create revenue was $157M, up 14% excluding a one-time item. Growth came from price increases, minimum commitments and China, where Create grew almost 50% in FY2025 [Q4'25 call]. New Unity sign-ups rose 20% quarter over quarter in Q1'26.
What to monitor
Create growth excluding one-time items, Unity 7 adoption, and how much of Create growth comes from price versus volume.
A4Growth turns into GAAP profit and per-share value
What must happen
Margins need to keep expanding while stock comp and the share count fall, so free cash flow per share compounds.
Why Unity specifically
Incremental margins are high because the ad business sits on existing infrastructure. Exiting ironSource and Supersonic removes low-margin revenue. Management expects Supersonic's exit alone to add at least 200bp of operating margin [Q1'26 call].
Evidence
Adjusted EBITDA margin was 29% in Q2'26, up 800bp, with 33% guided for Q3. Q2 free cash flow was $202M, and H1 was $268M [10-Q]. Stock comp fell to 14% of revenue from 21% of FY2025 revenue [10-K]. Management expects its first GAAP profit in Q3'26. The $558M 2026 convertible will be repaid in cash in November.
What to monitor
GAAP net income, stock comp as a % of revenue, diluted share count, and recurring restructuring charges.

vs
B1Attack

Vector's lead is a model lead, and advertisers can leave the next day

Advertisers put budget wherever ROAS is best each day, with no contract and no integration lock-in. The 10-K names AppLovin and Moloco as competitors, and both run the same kind of self-learning bidding models. Some of Vector's 80% year-over-year growth (Q1'26) was recovery from a weak base, and that part won't repeat.

Confirms failure: Vector sequential growth below about 10% for two quarters, Q3/Q4 guidance cut, or management moving attention from Vector growth to 'platform' metrics.

Permanent if a rival's model lead widens, because ad-network scale reinforces itself in both directions.

A2Bull assumption

Runtime data is a signal rivals can't buy

Player-behaviour data from the Unity runtime needs to make Vector's predictions measurably better, and developers need to keep opting in.

Evidence: Runtime signals went into production models only at the end of Q2'26, so this is still early. Opt-in to the developer data framework is above 90% [Q1'26 call].

vs
B2Attack

The runtime 'moat' depends on permission Unity doesn't control

The data belongs to developers' players and reaches Vector only through developer opt-in, OS policy and privacy law. The 10-K flags Apple's tracking opt-out, planned Android controls, child-protection rules and a new DOJ restriction on sensitive-data flows. Big studios may also object to their players' behaviour training a network that sells installs to their competitors.

Confirms failure: Opt-in falling below 90%, an Apple/Google SDK or data-policy change, or regulatory action on in-game data used for advertising.

Permanent. Platform policy changes rarely reverse.

A3Bull assumption

The engine stays the default through the AI transition and feeds the ad flywheel

Unity needs to hold its roughly 70% share of mobile game creation [Q1'26 call] as coding agents change how games get built. Pricing needs to move from seats to agent usage and consumption without revenue leaking out.

Evidence: Strategic Create revenue was $157M, up 14% excluding a one-time item. Growth came from price increases, minimum commitments and China, where Create grew almost 50% in FY2025 [Q4'25 call]. New Unity sign-ups rose 20% quarter over quarter in Q1'26.

vs
B4Attack

AI weakens the seat-based engine business and shrinks the top of the funnel

If coding agents and world models do the work that seats used to do, fewer paid seats are needed for each game. Unity is opening its APIs and MCP server for free. Recent Create growth has come from price increases and minimum commitments, which have limits. Q3 Create is guided to +7–10%, down from 14%.

Confirms failure: Create growth excluding one-time items falling to low single digits, agent-based pricing failing to show up in revenue, or rival engines or AI-native tools taking mobile share.

Partly fixable if consumption pricing works. Permanent if the engine becomes a commodity layer.

A4Bull assumption

Growth turns into GAAP profit and per-share value

Margins need to keep expanding while stock comp and the share count fall, so free cash flow per share compounds.

Evidence: Adjusted EBITDA margin was 29% in Q2'26, up 800bp, with 33% guided for Q3. Q2 free cash flow was $202M, and H1 was $268M [10-Q]. Stock comp fell to 14% of revenue from 21% of FY2025 revenue [10-K]. Management expects its first GAAP profit in Q3'26. The $558M 2026 convertible will be repaid in cash in November.

vs
B3Attack

Headline profit flatters per-share economics, and the capital-allocation record is poor

Adjusted EBITDA excludes stock comp ($385M in FY2025) and restructuring charges that recur every year: $267M in 2024, $47M in 2025, $38M in H1'26. ironSource cost $2.9B in 2022 [FY22 10-K]. Its ad network has been shut down with a $279M impairment, and $3.16B of goodwill remains. The useful lives of the Wētā intangibles were cut to 1–3 years. In February management said ironSource would 'get smaller and smaller over time', then announced its shutdown six weeks later.

Confirms failure: Restructuring charges continuing in FY2027, stock comp moving back above 15% of revenue, or another impairment.

Fixable. Stock comp is already falling. But each new write-down weakens the case that management allocates capital well.

Why the market might be missing it: the consolidated numbers hid Vector until this year. FY2025 revenue grew 2% with a $401M net loss, and H1'26 GAAP results included a $279M impairment. Q3'26 is the first quarter with almost no non-strategic revenue, and the first where the reported numbers show what the business actually earns.

3. Bear case — Munger invert

The most likely way I lose money is that Vector turns out to be a catch-up spike in a model race with no switching costs. Its growth reverts toward market growth, and a stock priced for compounding is left with a mid-growth engine business and a history of write-downs.

6. Bottom line

  1. Unity could work because Vector has turned the engine's reach into a 60%+ growth ad business on 83% adjusted gross margins, and exiting ironSource and Supersonic now lets that growth show up in reported profit.
  2. Vector needs to keep beating rival bidding models for several more quarters, runtime data needs to become a lasting edge that developers keep opting into, and Unity 7 needs to keep the engine as the default entry point as AI changes how games are built.
  3. The most important way the thesis breaks is that Vector's lead turns out to be a temporary model advantage in a market with no switching costs, which would bring growth down toward market rates while a record of write-downs and dilution remains.
  4. I would change my mind if Vector's sequential growth fell below about 10% for two straight quarters, or if developer opt-in or platform policy took away the runtime data advantage.
