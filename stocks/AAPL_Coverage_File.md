## 0. 30-second reopen

- **Business:** Sells premium hardware — iPhone above all — into a large installed base, then monetizes that base through high-margin Services; revenue is TTM $466.8bn with Services $120.5bn of it.
- **Core debate:** Is FY2026's 16% revenue surge and 49% gross margin a durable AI-driven upgrade cycle plus permanent mix shift, or a pulled-forward iPhone wave sitting on a one-off tariff refund just as memory costs turn?

- **Main bear case:** Services' most profitable dollars (Google search licensing, App Store commissions) are under simultaneous legal attack, while memory inflation and AI compute spend reverse the FY2026 margin gain.
- **What would change my mind:** Services growth decelerating below ~10%, products gross margin falling back below 37%, or an adverse final outcome on the Google licensing arrangement.
- **Open flags:** New CEO John Ternus since September 1, 2026; inventories nearly doubled; gross intangibles up $13.3bn in nine months — all unexplained in the filings.

## 1. Business snapshot

Apple designs and sells consumer hardware and monetizes the resulting installed base through services. In FY2025, Products were $307.0bn of $416.2bn of net sales and Services $109.2bn [FY2025 10-K]. Within Products, iPhone alone was $209.6bn — half of total company revenue — with Mac $33.7bn, iPad $28.0bn and Wearables, Home and Accessories $35.7bn.

Services is a bundle: advertising (including third-party search licensing), the App Store and other digital content, cloud services, AppleCare, and payment services [FY2025 10-K]. Its economics differ sharply from hardware — 75.4% gross margin in FY2025 against 36.8% for Products — so Services generated 42% of company gross profit on 26% of revenue.

Apple designs "nearly the entire solution" — hardware, operating system, applications and services — but manufactures almost nothing: substantially all hardware is built by outsourcing partners located primarily in China mainland, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and Vietnam [FY2025 10-K]. Reporting is geographic (Americas, Europe including India/MEA, Greater China, Japan, Rest of Asia Pacific); FY2025 distribution ran 40% direct, 60% indirect.

**Latest period.** For the nine months ended June 27, 2026, net sales were $364.4bn, up 16%, with iPhone up 22% to $196.5bn and Services up 14% to $91.7bn [Q3 FY2026 10-Q]. Greater China, which fell 4% in FY2025 and 8% in FY2024, rose 30% over the same nine months. Total gross margin reached 49.1%, versus 46.8% a year earlier.

## 2. Core debate

FY2026 broke a pattern. FY2024 and FY2025 were low-growth years — 2% and 6% — with iPhone roughly flat. Then iPhone grew 23%, 22% and 22% in the three quarters of FY2026, and gross margin expanded 230bp. The debate is what that inflection actually is.

**Structural reading:** Siri AI, introduced in the June 2026 quarter alongside iOS 27 [Q3 FY2026 10-Q], gave a very large installed base a reason to upgrade, and the Pro-model mix that drove every quarter's iPhone growth is a durable price/mix gain. Services momentum plus richer mix reset earnings power upward.

**Optical reading:** part of the margin gain is not operating at all. On February 20, 2026 the Supreme Court struck down certain IEEPA tariffs; Apple applied for refunds and recognized them **as a reduction of products cost of sales** [Q3 FY2026 10-Q]. Management attributes the products margin increase to "a different mix of products and tariff refunds." The same filing warns Apple is "experiencing a period of supply constraints and increasing costs" for advanced semiconductors, NAND and DRAM, and "expects these trends to intensify." On this reading, a pulled-forward wave plus a non-recurring credit flatter a business about to face cost inflation and hard FY2027 comparisons.

The filings do not settle it: Apple discloses neither the refund amount nor units, ASPs, installed base or subscriber counts, so the split cannot be computed from sources.

## 3. Base thesis

Apple's durable asset is an installed base it monetizes repeatedly at very high incremental margin, plus a capital structure that converts even flat operating economics into rising per-share value. Hardware growth over FY2023–FY2025 was close to zero; Services grew 13%, 14% and 14% again through nine months of FY2026, at gross margins rising from 70.8% to 76.3%. That mix shift is why company gross margin climbed from 44.1% to 46.9% with almost no revenue growth. On top of it, ~$137bn of TTM free cash flow funds a buyback that retired 5.1% of diluted shares between FY2023 and FY2025 and continues at $62.1bn over nine months of FY2026. FY2026 adds a genuine hardware cycle to that machine.

**Assumptions this rests on:**

1. Services compounds in the low-to-mid teens, and its ~76% gross margin holds — no adverse regulatory outcome forces a material change to App Store commissions or the search licensing arrangement.
2. The installed base is sticky enough that hardware share loss is gradual, so each upgrade cycle re-monetizes broadly the same customers at flat-to-higher ASPs.
3. Free cash flow conversion stays near current levels — capex has stayed light ($10.0bn TTM against $146.7bn of operating cash flow) even as AI investment scales.
4. Capital returns continue at roughly the current pace; the Board authorized another $100bn of repurchases on April 30, 2026 and raised the dividend to $0.27 [Q2 FY2026 10-Q].
5. The FY2026 gross margin level does not fully revert as tariff refunds lapse and memory costs rise.

## 4. Main bear case

Apple's most profitable dollars are legally contingent, and its cost base is turning at the wrong moment. Services carries 40% of gross profit, and two of its largest contributors are under direct attack. Apple "earns revenue from licensing arrangements with Google LLC... to offer their search services on the Company's platforms"; Google was found to have violated US antitrust laws (August 5, 2024), remedies were ordered September 2, 2025, and both DOJ and Google appealed — a reversal "could result in imposition of certain remedies initially proposed by the DOJ, such as those prohibiting Google from offering the Company commercial terms for search distribution" [Q3 FY2026 10-Q]. Separately, the Epic injunction constrains App Store commissions and the EU's DMA Article 6(4) investigation carries fines of up to 10% of worldwide net sales.

Meanwhile the cost side inflects: R&D rose 32% in the June quarter, to 11% of sales from 9%, on "higher infrastructure-related costs, including investments in artificial intelligence"; a new risk factor warns Apple "may be unable to secure sufficient capacity on commercially reasonable terms" for AI compute; memory inflation is expected to intensify. Gross intangibles rose $13.3bn and inventories nearly doubled to $11.1bn in nine months, neither explained.

**How it breaks:** the FY2026 upgrade wave pulls demand forward into a difficult FY2027 comparison; memory costs and AI infrastructure spend compress margins back toward FY2025 levels; and an adverse Google or App Store outcome removes high-margin Services revenue with no offsetting cost. Earnings then fall on multiple fronts at once, against a multiple that assumes the opposite.

## 5. Business quality

High, with a specific dependency. Evidence for quality: 49.1% gross margin at nine months FY2026 on $364bn of revenue; $146.7bn TTM operating cash flow against only $10.0bn of capex — enormous cash generation without owning factories; and the ability to raise price/mix through Pro models three quarters running. Shareholders' equity of $107.5bn against $383.3bn of assets reflects decades of capital returned, not weakness: Apple holds $146.5bn of cash and securities against $84.3bn of debt.

The dependency is concentration. iPhone is 53% of TTM revenue, and Services — the profit engine — rests partly on a contract with a competitor (Google) and on a commission structure under court order. Apple itself notes it holds "a minority market share in the global smartphone, personal computer, tablet and wearables markets" [FY2025 10-K]: quality comes from the profit pool it captures, not from units.

## 6. Key value drivers

- **iPhone Pro mix.** Every FY2026 quarter attributed iPhone growth specifically to Pro models — price/mix, not units.
- **Services growth and margin.** +14% at 76.3% gross margin over 9M FY2026, driven by advertising and cloud services.
- **Greater China.** From −8% (FY2024) and −4% (FY2025) to +30% over 9M FY2026, aided by a stronger renminbi.
- **Gross margin durability.** Products 39.9% vs 36.9% — how much survives lapsing tariff refunds and rising memory costs.
- **Buyback pace.** $62.1bn over 9M FY2026, with $63.8bn remaining at Q2 plus a new $100bn authorization.
- **AI investment intensity.** R&D at 11% of sales in Q3 FY2026 — step-change or permanent new cost floor.

## 9. Management & capital allocation

**Apple is days into a CEO transition.** On April 20, 2026 the Board announced Tim Cook would move to Executive Chair and appointed John Ternus, previously SVP of Hardware Engineering, as CEO — both effective September 1, 2026; Art Levinson moved from Chair to Lead Independent Director [8-K 2026-04-20]. This caps a broader refresh: Sabih Khan COO (July 2025), Kevan Parekh CFO, Jennifer Newstead General Counsel (March 2026), Ben Borders Principal Accounting Officer (January 2026). A hardware engineer now runs a company whose profit growth is increasingly a services-and-software story.

Compensation is TSR-anchored: performance RSUs vest on three-year total shareholder return relative to the S&P 500. FY2022 grants vested at 187% of target on 81.20th-percentile relative TSR, and FY2025 results produced maximum cash-incentive payouts; say-on-pay support was 92% [2026 DEF 14A]. Ternus's FY2027 target equity is $55m (75% performance-based), Cook's $45m (50%) [8-K/A 2026-09-01]. Relative-TSR-only vesting rewards share-price outperformance without requiring returns on invested capital — worth watching as AI spending scales.

Capital allocation is consistent and return-heavy: $90.7bn of buybacks and $15.4bn of dividends in FY2025, a further $100bn authorization on April 30, 2026, and the dividend raised to $0.27. Capex stays strikingly light relative to cash generation.

*Claims to verify over time:* whether Ternus's product-engineering background changes R&D allocation or the pace of the AI build; whether the doubled inventory and the $13.3bn intangibles increase reflect memory pre-buying and AI licensing (both plausible, neither disclosed).

## 10. Risks ranked by damage

### Risk 1 — Loss or repricing of the Google search licensing arrangement
**Damage rank:** High · **Permanence:** High · **Fixability:** Low

*Mechanism:* Licensing revenue from Google for default search placement carries near-100% incremental margin; losing it would flow almost entirely to operating income.
*Evidence today:* Google was found to have violated US antitrust laws (August 5, 2024); the DC District Court ordered remedies September 2, 2025; both DOJ and Google appealed. A reversal could impose remedies "prohibiting Google from offering the Company commercial terms for search distribution" [Q3 FY2026 10-Q].
*What would confirm it:* An appellate ruling reinstating the DOJ's original remedies, or Services deceleration coinciding with a change in the arrangement.
*Impact on value:* Not disclosed, so it cannot be sized from sources — which is itself the problem. The largest unquantifiable item in this file.

### Risk 2 — Regulatory compression of App Store economics
**Damage rank:** High · **Permanence:** High · **Fixability:** Medium

*Mechanism:* Commissions on third-party transactions are high-margin Services revenue. Court orders and the DMA force alternative distribution and payment, narrowing what Apple can charge.
*Evidence today:* The 2025 Injunction barred commissions on out-of-app purchases; the Ninth Circuit modified it December 11, 2025 to permit some commission on link-outs plus parity requirements; the Supreme Court granted certiorari June 30, 2026 on the civil contempt standard. The EU fined Apple €500m under DMA Article 5(4) in April 2025 (under appeal); Article 6(4) findings carry fines up to 10% of worldwide net sales [Q3 FY2026 10-Q].
*What would confirm it:* An adverse final Supreme Court or district court outcome, or a DMA Article 6(4) violation finding.
*Impact on value:* Direct reduction of the highest-margin Services line, plus DMA interoperability costs Apple says have already caused it not to launch features such as Siri AI in some jurisdictions.

### Risk 3 — Component cost inflation reversing the FY2026 margin gain
**Damage rank:** Medium-High · **Permanence:** Low-Medium · **Fixability:** Medium

*Mechanism:* Apple buys memory, storage and advanced semiconductors it does not make; supply-demand imbalance raises input costs, and offsetting price increases can reduce demand.
*Evidence today:* Apple says it is "experiencing a period of supply constraints and increasing costs" for NAND, DRAM and advanced semiconductors and "expects these trends to intensify" [Q3 FY2026 10-Q]. Products margin already reflects "higher costs, including memory," offset by mix and tariff refunds. Inventories nearly doubled to $11.1bn.
*What would confirm it:* Products gross margin declining sequentially despite favorable mix.
*Impact on value:* Cyclical rather than permanent — but it lands on a valuation priced for the FY2026 margin level.

### Risk 4 — iPhone demand pull-forward
**Damage rank:** Medium · **Permanence:** Low · **Fixability:** Low

*Mechanism:* An AI-driven upgrade wave compresses several years of replacement into FY2026, leaving a demand air pocket.
*Evidence today:* iPhone grew 22–23% for three consecutive quarters against a flat FY2025 base. Apple discloses no units, ASPs or installed-base data with which to distinguish penetration from acceleration.
*What would confirm it:* iPhone growth turning negative in FY2027 without a competitive or macro explanation.
*Impact on value:* One or two weak years; does not impair the franchise.

### Risk 5 — Manufacturing and geographic concentration
**Damage rank:** Medium · **Permanence:** Medium · **Fixability:** Low

*Mechanism:* Substantially all hardware is assembled by outsourcing partners concentrated in Asia, and custom components often come from a single source; tariffs, export controls or geopolitical disruption hit cost and availability directly.
*Evidence today:* $57.0bn of manufacturing purchase obligations at June 27, 2026; an open Section 232 semiconductor investigation whose January 2026 initial results imposed no new tariffs; new Section 301 tariffs [Q3 FY2026 10-Q]. Greater China is both a large market (~15% of sales) and a manufacturing base.
*What would confirm it:* Reinstated or expanded tariffs on Apple products, or export restrictions on key components.
*Impact on value:* Margin pressure and supply disruption; the FY2026 refund shows how fast this line swings both ways.

### Risk 6 — AI execution and compute dependency
**Damage rank:** Medium · **Permanence:** Medium · **Fixability:** Medium

*Mechanism:* If AI features underdeliver, the upgrade rationale weakens; if compute cannot be secured economically, Apple either degrades features or absorbs higher cost.
*Evidence today:* A new risk factor discloses reliance on third-party cloud providers and that Apple "may be unable to secure sufficient capacity on commercially reasonable terms, or at all"; R&D rose 32% year-over-year on AI infrastructure [Q3 FY2026 10-Q].
*What would confirm it:* R&D and infrastructure costs outgrowing revenue without a matching Services or hardware response.
*Impact on value:* Erodes the operating leverage that has made Apple's growth so profitable.

## 13. Open questions

1. **How large is the Google licensing revenue?** Nowhere in sources, yet the largest unquantified risk in this file. Any external figure is an estimate.
2. **How much of the products margin gain is tariff refunds versus mix?** Management names both; sources quantify neither.
3. **What drove gross intangibles from $25.0bn to $38.2bn in nine months** — AI or content licensing, an acquisition, something else? *(Verification path in §9.)*
4. **Is the doubled inventory a deliberate memory pre-buy or a demand misjudgment?** The former is prudent; the latter is a warning.
5. **Where does capex go?** $10.0bn TTM looks low for a company calling itself compute-constrained; the doubling of other purchase obligations to $29.3bn may be where the AI commitment sits.
6. **What changes under Ternus?** No strategic disclosure yet; first evidence is the FY2026 10-K and the FY2027 cycle.
